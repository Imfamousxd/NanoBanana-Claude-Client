## UGC testimonial / talking-head (organic, phone-shot, creator-style)
_A single-person, phone-shot-looking video where a believable "real user" talks to camera about a product — engineered to read as an organic post a friend filmed from their couch, not a branded ad, and to survive as paid social creative._

**Highest-impact levers:** The first 2-3s hook (visual + verbal together) — single biggest driver of watch-through and the fastest to fatigue; Perceived production level — polish is inversely correlated with authenticity; the ceiling on 'niceness' is a lever, not a floor; Script structure, specifically the presence of a 'failed attempts' beat and a soft vs hard CTA; Delivery cadence and imperfection (words/sec, filler, running sentences together) — the difference between 'friend' and 'ad read'; Face/skin realism and framing tightness — texture, real light, hairline crop; the single-take, no-caption, no-logo discipline; Hook variation VOLUME in the ad account — how many distinct openers you rotate before the creative dies

**Best practices:**
- Front-load a hook in the first 2-3 seconds that combines a VISUAL grab (unexpected motion/framing) with a spoken pain-point, bold claim, or result — the hook decides whether anything after it is seen; target a 3-second hook rate >35% before scaling spend.
- Structure the script as hook -> problem -> failed attempts -> discovery -> result -> soft CTA. The 'failed attempts' beat ('I tried X and Y, nothing worked...') is the specific thing that makes it believable; without it the ad reads too clean.
- Aim for 'structured spontaneity': every beat is scripted, but delivery is loose, imperfect, runs sentences together, and occasionally rushes a word. Perfect delivery reads as 'ad'.
- Shoot ONE unbroken take, no cutaways or b-roll inserts in the UGC lane — one person, one continuous shot, their own hands. (House-measured: the 7-shot v1 explainer was revised down to a single-take v2.)
- Frame it as an extreme selfie close-up: face fills the frame edge-to-edge, cropped at the hairline, eyes in the upper third, defocused real-world background. A medium/wide shot reads as a different, more produced format.
- Keep skin and light unflattering and specific — visible pores, oil shine, freckles/flush, blown highlights where sun clips one side, flyaway hair in wind. No beauty filter, no smoothing, no colour grade. Texture is the authenticity tell.
- End on a SOFT, peer-to-peer CTA ('check it out if you're dealing with the same thing'), never 'BUY NOW'. An aggressive CTA breaks the authenticity every prior beat built.
- Deliver 9:16 vertical, full-bleed, so it sits native in-feed; the goal is content indistinguishable from an organic post, which is also what the algorithm boosts.
- Pace dialogue at real conversational speed. House-approved single-take UGC runs ~2.2-3.1 words/sec (median ~2.3) — underfilling a segment makes it sound like a prepared statement, not a friend talking.
- Never name a device or 'screen/phone camera' in an AI prompt to get the look — it renders the phone mockup/bezel. Say 'casual handheld selfie, full-bleed, fills the frame edge to edge'.
- Combat creative fatigue by testing 20-50 hook variations programmatically against the SAME body, rather than re-rolling one hook — the hook is the highest-variance lever and burns out fastest.
- For AI-generated UGC, carry identity with anchored reference images every generation and animate a talking FACE (not hands manipulating a product) — object permanence in hands is a known video-model failure that destroys the 'real' illusion.

**Judge rubric (PASS / FAIL):**

| Criterion | PASS | FAIL |
|---|---|---|
| Hook lands in first ~3s | A visual grab + a verbal pain-point/claim/result both hit within the first 2-3 seconds | Opens on a logo, product beauty shot, slow pan, or the pitch takes >4s to start |
| Single continuous take | One person, one unbroken shot, no cutaways or b-roll inserts | Multi-shot montage, jump cuts, or inserted product b-roll in the talking-head lane |
| Selfie-tight vertical framing | 9:16, face fills frame cropped near the hairline, eyes upper third, defocused real background | Medium/wide framing, horizontal, mockup bezel, or studio backdrop |
| Unpolished realism | Visible skin texture, real/uneven light, no beauty smoothing or colour grade, natural imperfection | Retouched skin, even studio key light, glossy grade, filtered/AI-smooth face |
| Peer-tone script with a discovery arc | Feels like a friend recommending; includes problem and/or failed-attempt before the result | Feature-listing pitch, brand voice, or 'clean' claim with no lived-experience framing |
| Soft CTA | Recommendation-style close ('worth a look if...'), conversational | Hard imperative ('Buy now', 'Order today', urgency/discount shouting') |
| No ad furniture | No burned-in captions, no logo bug, no on-screen text; only brand surface is a product/card in hand | Branded lower-thirds, logo watermark, kinetic caption package, price/promo overlays |
| Conversational cadence | ~2.2-3.5 words/sec with natural filler and run-on delivery | Slow, even, monotone narration that reads as a prepared statement or announcer read |

## Static ad creative (paid social / Meta ad image)
_A single still image built to stop the scroll and convert on Meta feeds/placements, where composition and one text hook do all the work motion normally does — a dominant subject, one claim/offer, and a placement-correct crop._

**Highest-impact levers:** Aspect ratio / crop discipline — 4:5 master + placement-correct 9:16 and 1:1 exports; wrong ratio silently caps delivery and wastes the frame; Visual hierarchy — a single dominant subject/hook the eye lands on first; the biggest single quality driver in a still where nothing moves; The claim/offer itself — one specific, outcome-tied statement or a concrete number, vs a vague feature/benefit adjective; Foreground-to-background luminance/colour contrast — the motion substitute that creates the thumb-stop; Format choice matched to funnel stage — problem-solution/before-after for cold, benefit-stack/product+price for retarget; On-image text load & safe-zone placement — text ≤~1/3 area, hook in the centre third, chrome zones left clear

**Best practices:**
- Build the master at 4:5, 1080x1350px, for feed. It occupies the most vertical mobile real estate and measurably out-CTRs 1:1 and 16:9. Export 9:16 (1080x1920) for Stories/Reels and 1:1 (1080x1080) for square-safe placements from the same idea — never ship 16:9 or 2:1 landscape to feed (our Muha group-truck 2x1 is key art, not a feed ad).
- Treat the static's 'hook' as the visual hierarchy of the first frame: within ~500ms the dominant element must answer both 'what is this' and 'this is for someone like me' before a word is read. One clear focal point, not two competing ones.
- Substitute contrast for motion. High luminance contrast between a foreground subject and its background creates the pop that motion gives video — but the contrast must MEAN something (a before/after, product-in-use, a face reacting), because decorative contrast underperforms.
- Lead problem-aware, product-second for cold traffic. The safest cold formats are problem-solution and before-after: tell the buyer why to care before asking for attention. Reserve product-forward/benefit-stack statics (price + availability visible) for retargeting and marketplace, where the product IS the message.
- Make one specific claim tied to a concrete outcome, not a feature list. 'Cut weekly creative from 6 hours to 45 minutes' beats 'Love it'; a social-proof NUMBER ('47,000 teams…') anchors credibility better than an adjective.
- Keep on-image text to roughly one-third of the area or less. The old 20% rule is gone, but lighter-text creatives still get broader delivery and better results; put long-form persuasion in the caption (1-2 sentences, under ~125 characters before truncation), keep the core offer visible on the image.
- Respect Meta safe zones on vertical crops: leave ~14% clear at top and ~20-35% clear at bottom (that's where profile name, CTA button and swipe live). Keep the hook and key subject in the centre third so no placement's UI chrome eats them.
- Let gpt-image-2 render the whole creative including headline type — it sets small lowercase and big display type accurately (verified in our own DIALED/HEALTH and Muha 'WIN $25,000' work). Spell every on-image string exactly in the prompt. For Gemini/Nano Banana, keep text minimal and composite the flat headline/logo/QR plate onto a reserved clean area instead.
- Never instruct 'no logo' or leave a surface unspecified — an unspecified surface gets filled (a bare label grew an invented gold emblem). Specify exactly what belongs in every reserved zone: headline here, logo there, CTA plate bottom-third.
- Anchor product fidelity with the canonical packshot as a reference image and name its role ('reproduce the label exactly'); text-only prompts drift. Then rank candidates against the canonical with the product-lock rubric (--refs), never from memory.
- Generate 2-3 candidates and vary the LEVER, not the seed — change the light, the framing, the moment (studio hero vs hand-held-in-use vs before/after). Re-rolling the same prompt just resamples the model's average, and the average is the 'AI look'.
- Finish on a whole-frame grade, not in the prompt: neutralize the global colour cast (real capture has a white balance, not a wash) and add emulsion-style grain in post (film-grain.py). Both back-ends lay down a smooth clean cast that reads as 'generated'.

**Judge rubric (PASS / FAIL):**

| Criterion | PASS | FAIL |
|---|---|---|
| Placement-correct ratio | Delivered at 4:5 (1080x1350) for feed, or the exact target ratio (9:16 1080x1920 / 1:1 1080x1080) for the named placement | 16:9, 2:1 landscape, or an off-size crop (e.g. 3:4 / 860x1076) sent to feed |
| Single dominant focal point | One subject/element the eye lands on first within ~0.5s; hierarchy is unambiguous | Two or more elements compete, or the eye has no clear entry point |
| One specific claim or offer | A single outcome-tied claim or concrete number/offer is legible on the image | Feature list, vague adjective ('amazing quality'), or no discernible message |
| Contrast that carries meaning | Foreground pops from background AND the contrast communicates something (use/transformation/reaction) | Flat subject-background separation, or high contrast that is purely decorative |
| Text load & safe zones | On-image text ≤~1/3 area, hook in centre third, top ~14% and bottom ~20-35% clear of critical elements | Text-heavy plate, or hook/logo sitting where Stories/Reels UI chrome overlaps it |
| Text rendered legibly and spelled correctly | Every on-image string is sharp and correctly spelled (gpt-image-2 native, or a composited flat plate) | Garbled/misspelled type or legible-looking nonsense fine print from a Gemini render |
| Product/brand fidelity | Logo, label and colours match the canonical asset (anchored by reference image) | Invented emblem, wrong brand colours, or drifted label from a text-only prompt |
| Realism finish (non-lifestyle exception aside) | Global colour cast neutralized and emulsion grain added in post where the frame should read as captured | Smooth clean AI cast left in on a frame meant to look real |

## Promo / giveaway creative (prize, entry mechanic, urgency)
_A paid-social/e-commerce creative whose job is to convert a scroll into a giveaway/sweepstakes/raffle ENTRY by making one specific prize instantly desirable, one entry action instantly obvious, one deadline instantly felt — while carrying the legitimacy signals (real brand lockup, "official entry", no-purchase/rules disclosure) that separate a real promotion from a scam._

**Highest-impact levers:** Prize specificity + scale — exact named prize and dollar figure, shown as a real object, sized as the hero. Moves both desire and legitimacy simultaneously.; Legitimacy cluster — faithful brand lockup + 'official entry' badge + rules/no-purchase disclosure. Its ABSENCE on a slick creative is what flips it to 'scam'.; Entry-mechanic clarity + friction — is the ONE action to enter visible on the creative, and is the destination form near-zero-friction (2 fields).; Urgency with a concrete deadline — dated countdown/end date lifts entries and reads legit; vague/false urgency reads scam.; On-image text + logo fidelity — exact spellings and a faithfully reproduced wordmark; garbled type is the strongest visual scam tell.; Compositional hierarchy — three clean tiers (prize > badge+action > disclosure/urgency) so it stops the scroll AND is trusted at thumbnail size.

**Best practices:**
- Make the PRIZE the hero and name it exactly: brand + model + dollar value as the single largest element ('WIN $25,000 CASH + DODGE CHALLENGER', not 'win big'). Specificity is the #1 legitimacy signal — an unrealistic or vague prize reads as scam.
- Show the prize as a photoreal object, not a symbol. A rendered car + stacks of cash out-converts a gift-card icon or 'value' number alone; the viewer must picture owning the thing.
- State the ONE entry action in one line ('Scan to redeem 10 entries', 'Tap Learn More to enter', 'Follow + tag a friend'). A giveaway creative with no visible entry mechanic is a poster, not an ad — it wins awareness, not entries.
- Minimize entry friction on the destination: two-field form (name + email) is the high-converting standard; every extra required field or task ('download app, refer 10 friends, 20-page survey') drops completion and reads scammy.
- Carry the legitimacy cluster ON the creative: verified brand lockup/logo reproduced faithfully, an 'OFFICIAL ENTRY' or program-name badge ('MUHA MEMBERS RAFFLE'), and a rules/eligibility line. Polished graphics WITHOUT these read MORE scammy, not less.
- Put disclosure where it does double duty as trust: 'No Purchase Necessary', age gate (21+ for regulated goods), and an Official-Rules/AMOE reference. FTC requires it for chance-based sweeps and its presence is what real winners look for.
- Give urgency a REAL, dated deadline or countdown ('Ends Aug 31' / live timer), not adjectives. A concrete end date reads legit and lifts entries double digits in A/B tests; a 'HURRY 60 MINUTES!!!' with no date reads as a scam's false urgency.
- One CTA, one destination. A single low-friction action beats a menu of tasks; put the CTA above the fold on the landing page alongside prize headline, prize image, and a trust signal.
- Spell every on-image string exactly and reproduce the logo from the real file — misspellings, garbled logos, and mismatched fonts are the top visual scam tells. Never let the model invent the wordmark.
- Match prize scale to brand plausibility. A prize wildly beyond what the brand could credibly give (or grammar/branding inconsistencies) trips the 'too good to be true' filter; keep the value ambitious but believable and on-brand.
- Keep the hierarchy to three tiers: prize headline (biggest) > program/verification badge + entry action > disclosure/urgency (small but present). Clutter and competing type destroy both stopping power and trust.
- Design mobile-first and platform-native (9:16 / 4:5, prize legible at thumbnail size). 70-80% of paid-social performance is the creative; a prize headline that survives a phone thumbnail is the whole ballgame.

**Judge rubric (PASS / FAIL):**

| Criterion | PASS | FAIL |
|---|---|---|
| Prize is specific and hero | Exact prize + value is the largest element and shown as a photoreal object | Prize vague ('win big'), symbolic-only, or not the dominant element |
| Entry mechanic is visible | One clear action to enter appears on the creative or its CTA ('Scan to enter', 'Tap Learn More') | No indication of HOW to enter — a prize poster with no path to action |
| Legitimacy cluster present | Faithful brand lockup + an 'official entry'/program badge or verified identity cue | No brand verification cue, or a garbled/invented logo |
| Disclosure present | Carries 'No Purchase Necessary' and/or age gate and/or Official-Rules reference as legible copy | No disclosure of any kind on a chance-based promo |
| Urgency is concrete | A real dated deadline or countdown, or clearly deadline-driven framing | No deadline, or only vague/hyped urgency with no date |
| On-image text and logo are clean | All strings spelled correctly, brand wordmark faithful, consistent fonts | Any misspelling, garbled logo, or mismatched/inconsistent branding |
| Single low-friction CTA | One CTA to one destination; implied entry is simple | Multiple competing CTAs or a task list that raises friction |
| Thumbnail-legible hierarchy | Prize headline readable at phone-thumbnail size; three clean tiers | Cluttered/competing type; prize unreadable when small |

We lack a proven 'Ends [date]' / live-countdown treatment.; No on-image DISCLOSURE cluster on the hero posters. 'No Purchase Necessary' / 21+ / Official-Rules exists only on the QR raffle-back and in spec text, never on a scroll-stopping ad. Need a version where disclosure is legible and doubles as a trust cue.; No explicit ENTRY-MECHANIC ad — a creative that shows the one action to enter as its own beat ('Follow + tag', 'Scan to enter', a 3-step how-to). Current assets stop at 'LEARN MORE'.; No WINNER-PROOF / social-proof asset (past-winner photo, entry counter, 'X entered'). Public winner lists are a top legitimacy signal we produce nothing for.; No giveaway LANDING PAGE or destination artifact — the above-the-fold prize+form+trust+countdown page that the creative should route to (25-35% entry benchmark) is untested in our stack.; No VIDEO/UGC giveaway creative — no talking-head 'I just entered the Muha raffle' hook clip; all promo work is static. ## Commercial / brand video (cinematic ad)
_A short (15–30s) multi-shot, music-led brand film that sells a feeling and a product through cinematic shot grammar, a consistent grade, and beat-synced editing — distinct from the single-take UGC lane; it is the repo's "campaign/announce" lane._

**Highest-impact levers:** The 0–2s hook — product + tension in the opening frame decides the whole spend; Shot-scale variety and order — the establish→detail→reaction→hero ladder is what reads as 'production' vs 'clip'; Beat-synced edit rhythm — cutting to the track (~4.3s ASL) vs cutting on a stopwatch; The grade — one saturated, high-contrast, cast-neutralized look across every shot; Sound-off legibility — captions + a visual story that lands muted; The hero/beauty shot before the CTA and the brand-locked end card

**Best practices:**
- Front-load the hook: product + tension legible in the first 1.5–2s and in the opening frame, because 71% of viewers decide inside the first few seconds and paid-social feeds punish slow builds. This is the ONE place the campaign lane must beat the house UGC lane, which hooks late (median first-segment end 5.1s, house:hook-lands-later-than-the-generic-law) — that lateness is tolerable on a talking head, fatal on a scroll-stopping spot.
- Hold the 15–30s length. It is the measured survival band on paid social and it maps to the house duration ladder (5/10/30, house:duration-ladder-is-5-10-30) — there is no 20s rung, so ship 30s for the campaign lane, not an arbitrary length.
- Build from a shot ladder, not a single scale: at minimum one establishing/wide, one detail/macro insert, one human reaction close-up, one movement shot. ~71% of premium product videos use macro-to-wide scale transitions to 'prove' material/quality without copy.
- Cut on the music. The campaign lane's measured rhythm is ~4.3s average shot length across 7 shots over 30s (house:campaign-lane-cuts-every-4-3s); let beats — not a timer — trigger each cut, and reserve the strongest hero/beauty shot for just before the CTA.
- Design for sound-off first. 75%+ of mobile views are muted; the story must read with no audio via visual sequence and captions (captions lift brand recall ~58% and affinity ~95% on TikTok). Note this INVERTS the house UGC rule house:no-on-screen-text-in-the-ugc-lane — captions belong in the campaign lane, not the UGC lane.
- One grade across every shot. Advertising looks are more saturated and higher-contrast than documentary; the tell of amateur work is shots that don't match. Neutralize the per-back-end colour cast (Pattern F --neutralize) so the grade is a choice, not a wash.
- End on a brand-locked CTA card (final 2–4s): logo + one action. Do not leave the last surface bare — an unspecified surface gets filled with invented marks (golden rule 5).
- Prove the hero product on real brand art, not a video model's invention: composite the packshot/logo into a reserved clean plate (allowed exception to the never-paste rule) rather than asking the model to render fidelity it will drift on.
- Animate faces and simple camera moves, not hands manipulating products — object permanence in hands is a known Seedance failure (golden rule 10). For the macro/insert b-roll that must survive review, shoot it real or keep the motion minimal and single-action.
- Finish with the mandatory grain grade in post (heavy ≈ 18 0.7, medium ≈ 11 0.6). Neither image back-end produces film grain from a prompt; flat grain across the tone curve is the fake-grain tell (Pattern F, post_laws).
- Deliver at true 1080p by conforming with lanczos:param0=5, no sharpening, CRF 18, and upscale BEFORE upload (post:conform-720-to-1080-with-lanczos-p5-never-unsharp). Sharpening is a processed tell, not a fix.
- Keep audio levels honest: dialogue/VO at ~-16 LUFS, and level any quiet cinematic b-roll before cutting it against a voiced shot — the corpus b-roll sits 24dB quieter and drops the floor out if cut raw (house:broll-is-24db-quieter-than-dialogue).

**Judge rubric (PASS / FAIL):**

| Criterion | PASS | FAIL |
|---|---|---|
| Front-loaded hook | Product and a tension/benefit are legible within the first ~2s and present in the opening frame | Opens on logo/black/slow build; product or point not clear until after 3s |
| Length on the ladder | Total runtime 15–30s (30s for this repo's campaign lane) | Under 15s, over 30s, or an off-ladder ~20s that matches no shipped rung |
| Shot-grammar variety | ≥3 distinct shot scales including at least one wide/establishing AND one macro/detail insert | Single scale throughout (e.g. all medium), or no detail/macro insert |
| Grade consistency | One coherent graded look across all shots; global colour cast neutralized | Shots visibly mismatched in colour/contrast, or a raw generated wash left on |
| Sound-off legibility | Core message understandable muted via visual sequence and/or captions | Comprehension depends on VO/music; no captions and no self-evident visual story |
| Beat-synced pacing | Cuts land on musical beats; ASL roughly even (~3–5s) with a hero shot before the CTA | Cuts float against the track, or pacing is monotone/uncut where the lane demands multi-shot |
| Brand-locked CTA | Final 2–4s carry logo + one clear action; product fidelity from real brand art | No CTA/end card, bare unspecified surface, or hero fidelity faked/drifted by the model |
| No motion/finishing tells | Identity, product and objects hold across shots; delivered 1080p, grain in post, no over-sharpen | Morphing/object-permanence breaks in hands, 720p passthrough, or flat fake grain / over-sharpened edges |

## Product shot / packshot (studio product photography)
_A controlled studio photograph of a single product isolated on a clean or intentionally-designed surface, engineered so the item reads instantly, its label/brand is legible, and its form, material and color are truthful — spanning the e-commerce lane (pure-white, on-axis, catalog-consistent) and the hero lane (directional light, gradient/dark ground, dramatic separation)._

**Highest-impact levers:** Lane + background: pure-white 255 vs designed gradient/dark sweep — decides every other choice and whether the asset is even marketplace-legal.; Lighting ratio & direction: 45° key + 50–70% opposite fill + negative fill/black flags — the biggest separator of premium vs flat-frontal amateur, and the hardest thing for a text-only prompt to summon.; Reflection/hotspot control on glossy & glass: diffusion size (one clean reflected source), polarize/off-axis, flags — governs whether the surface reads as real material or plastic-flat.; Label legibility & product turn: angle-to-key, frame-fill %, on-axis vs 3/4 — the direct driver of trust and click-through, and where AI packshots most often fail (melted/misspelled type).; Grounding & retouch integrity: contact shadow/mirror reflection + dust/fingerprint/edge cleanup — the boundary between 'photographed' and 'composited/generated'.; Cross-SKU consistency: locked framing/scale/light so a variant swap is the only change — what makes a catalog look like one shoot instead of many rolls.

**Best practices:**
- Pick the LANE before lighting: e-commerce (marketplace/PDP) = pure white RGB 255,255,255, product on-axis filling ~85% of frame, no props, no text; hero (paid social/campaign) = gradient or dark sweep, directional key, negative space for copy. The two are different jobs and mixing them (dramatic hero shadows on an Amazon main image) gets the listing suppressed.
- Light with a key at ~45° to one side and a fill (or bounce card) at ~45° on the opposite side at 50–70% of key power. That ratio models the form — one lit side, one shadow side — instead of the flat, frontal, evenly-blown look that reads as amateur/AI.
- On glass and glossy surfaces, control reflections rather than kill them: diffuse the key large enough that its reflection is ONE clean soft gradient, and place a black flag/card close to the product on the side opposite the key. Negative fill is what draws the crisp dark edge on a vial — without it, glass goes milky and washed out.
- Legibility is the conversion lever, not drama. The primary brand name and the variant/flavor/dose must be fully sharp, correctly spelled, and unobstructed by hotspots. Turn the product a few degrees so the label faces the key and curves toward camera; if fine ingredient/legal type won't hold, compose it out of the hero read rather than shipping garbled type.
- Ground the product with a consistent contact shadow or a soft mirror reflection so it sits on a surface — or commit to a deliberate clean float. A hard-cutout object hovering in white space with a halo edge is the single loudest 'pasted-in' tell.
- Shoot for zoom and retouch-cleanliness: deliver ≥1600px on the long side so the PDP zoom works, and prep/retouch out dust, fingerprints, bent labels and lint — at zoom these are the difference between premium and amateur, and prep eliminates ~80% of retouch work.
- Neutralize the global color cast. Caps, liquid, powder and label color must be true to the physical product; a warm/cool wash over the whole frame is a generated-image giveaway and, for regulated goods, a compliance risk (color implies a claim).
- Hold consistency across a SKU set: identical camera height, distance, scale, background and light for every variant, with color-coded caps/labels as the ONLY thing that changes. A catalog reads as premium when 30 SKUs stack in a grid with zero drift in scale or lighting.
- Give the marketplace lane its secondary set: 3–4 additional angles per SKU (3/4 turn, back-of-pack, macro material/label crop, in-scale-of-hand) — these answer buyer questions and cut returns even though the white-BG main image wins the click.
- Frame with headroom and a level baseline: product centered, upright (use a plumb check), a small even margin all around, and nothing cropped at the frame edge on the e-commerce main image.
