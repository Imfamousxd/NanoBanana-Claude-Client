# TikTok Compliance Report: Peptides, GLP-1, and Supplements (Paid Ads, Shop, Organic, Branded Content)

**Prepared:** July 22, 2026. Prioritizes 2025–2026 policy state. Each claim is tagged as **[Official]** (TikTok policy text/help center), **[Practitioner]** (agency/consultant interpretation), or **[Journalism/Research]**.

## 1. Paid Ads — Industry Entry: Where Peptides, Injectables, Rx Drugs, Weight-Loss, and Supplements Fall

TikTok's ad rules live in the Advertising Policies "Industry Entry" hub, split by region (North America index: https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-advertising-policies-industry-entry-north-america; global overview: https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-advertising-policies-industry-entry). Relevant sub-policies: Healthcare & Pharmaceuticals, Weight Management, Dangerous Products or Services, Misinformation.

### Healthcare & Pharmaceuticals policy — https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-healthcare-pharmaceuticals **[Official]**

- **Prescription medicines: prohibited in nearly all markets.** A narrow carve-out exists only where advertisers "provide proof of approval and certification from appropriate local regional regulating authorities" (in practice: certified pharma/pharmacy advertisers via managed accounts; not self-serve). Practitioner guides confirm Rx ads are "generally prohibited in most markets" (https://ecomdymedia.com/blog/tiktok-healthcare-advertising-policy-2026, https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/insights/guide-to-social-media-health-ad-restrictions-2026/) **[Practitioner]**.
- **Injectables:** Botox is explicitly prohibited in most markets; invasive cosmetic procedures prohibited in most developed markets. Some non-invasive clinic services (dermal fillers, PRP by registered clinics, 18+) are conditionally allowed in some markets — but this is for licensed clinics, not products. There is no lawful path for advertising an injectable peptide product.
- **OTC medicines / medical devices:** restricted — allowed only with regulatory proof (FDA approval, CE/UKCA marking in EU/UK) and 18+ targeting.
- **Healthcare supplements:** generally allowed with 18+ restriction and regulatory approval; some LatAm markets require a health-risk disclosure.
- **Medical claims ban (all products):** "Medicinal claims to treat, cure, heal, or prevent any medical condition, disease, or symptoms" are prohibited; herbal remedies claiming to cure illness are prohibited outright.
- The policy does not name GLP-1/semaglutide explicitly — GLP-1 drugs are captured by the prescription-medicine prohibition and (for the copycat/supplement angle) by the weight-management rules below.

### Where each of the three client types lands **[Practitioner synthesis of the official text]**

| Category | Paid ads status |
|---|---|
| RUO peptide vials (BPC-157, semaglutide, tirzepatide "for research") | **Prohibited.** They are either unapproved new drugs (no regulatory approval to show) or "research chemicals" under Dangerous Products (https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-dangerous-products-or-services). No certification path exists. |
| GLP-1 weight-loss anything (drug, "GLP-1 supplement," patch, "nature's Ozempic") | **Prohibited** — Rx drug ban + weight-management supplement ban. |
| Weight-loss supplements (fat burners, appetite suppressants, detox teas) | **Prohibited in US/EU/UK** (see §2). |
| General dietary supplements (vitamins, protein, probiotics, non-weight-loss wellness) | **Restricted-allowed:** 18+ targeting, no disease/weight claims, and since the January 2025 policy update, pre-approval with documentation (cGMP cert per 21 CFR 111/117, Supplement Facts label images, facility registration, lab test report) (https://exitstrategyads.com/supplement-advertising-compliance-resource-center-ftc-fda-google-meta-tiktok-youtube-policies-2025-guide/, https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-advertise-supplements-on-tiktok) **[Practitioner]**. |

Note: one peptide-industry marketing guide (https://yourpeptidebrand.com/advanced-tiktok-ad-strategies-for-peptide-brands/) claims RUO peptide ads can run with "Research Use Only – Not for Human Consumption" disclaimers and TikTok pre-approval. **Treat this as aspirational vendor content, not policy** — it contradicts TikTok's published prohibitions, and FDA's March 2026 warning letters explicitly rejected RUO disclaimers as a shield (see §6). **[Practitioner — unreliable]**

## 2. Weight-Loss and Body-Image Ad Rules

### Official policy — Weight Management: https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-weight-management **[Official]**

- Since **September 2020**, TikTok globally banned ads for **fasting apps and weight-loss supplements** and restricted all weight-management ads to 18+ (contemporaneous coverage: https://www.cnbc.com/2020/09/23/tiktok-bans-ads-for-fasting-apps-dieting-products.html, https://www.forbes.com/sites/carlieporterfield/2020/09/23/tiktok-takes-on-body-shaming-with-a-ban-on-ads-for-fasting-apps/) **[Journalism]**. That ban remains the baseline in 2026.
- Current policy text (all regions): no "references to unrealistic muscle gain or weight loss," no "encouragement of unhealthy lifestyles or promotion of unhealthy relationships with food," no claims a product works "without diet or exercise," no "suggestions that losing weight or gaining muscles is easy or guaranteed," and "We also do not allow content that promotes disordered eating and risky weight management behaviors."
- Body-image rules: ads must not "explicitly shame users about their bodies, suggest there is an ideal body type," or tie appearance to confidence/success/popularity.
- All weight-loss/muscle-gain claims: **18+ only** and framed "via the promotion of a healthy lifestyle."
- Prohibited product list (Industry Entry text): "Ingestible products claiming to burn fat or help to lose or gain weight... (e.g. fat burning pills, appetite suppressants, weight loss, detox teas, or lollipops)" and "weight loss or management fasting products or services."

### Regional differences **[Official + Practitioner]**

- The current Weight Management page contains **carve-outs only for Asia-Pacific and MEA** (e.g., weight-loss supplements and meal replacements allowed with restrictions in AU/TH/VN/PH/SG; meal replacements in UAE/Egypt/Pakistan/Nigeria; weight-loss surgery only with TikTok sales-rep approval). **There is no US/Canada/EU/UK allowance section — in those markets weight-loss supplements, clinics, and fasting products remain prohibited from ads.**
- **Before/after imagery:** organic/commerce policy prohibits before-and-after weight transformation content and testimonials implying a product caused weight change (see §3 seller content rules); practitioner consensus is that before/after weight imagery in ads is rejected in US/EU **[Practitioner]**.
- **UK/EU extra layer:** advertising prescription-only medicines (all GLP-1 injections) to the public is illegal under UK law regardless of TikTok policy; the ASA/MHRA/GPhC issued a joint Enforcement Notice (Jan 2025), found ~1,800 unique paid weight-loss POM ads in a single month, and in December 2025 banned social ads from pharmacies Chequp, SkinnyJab, and MedExpress, plus the first bans of ordinary users'/influencers' discount-code posts (https://www.marketing-beat.co.uk/2025/12/17/asa-weightloss-ads/, https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/weight-control-prescription-only-medicines.html, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/advertising-investigations-december-2025/medicinal-treatment-services-for-weight-loss-december-2025) **[Official regulator + Journalism]**.

## 3. TikTok Shop — Supplements: Requirements, Bans, Enforcement Wave

### What's required to list supplements — Dietary Supplements Requirements: https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=1411277869713198&lang=en **[Official]**

- Supplements are an **invite-only / category-qualification** vertical ("Contact your Account Manager").
- Manufacturers/importers/repackers must submit: (1) **FDA facility registration proof** matching the label; (2) **cGMP certification** to 21 CFR Part 111; (3) **lab test report ≤180 days old** from an accredited lab (Eurofins, NSF, SGS, UL, TÜV SÜD, Mérieux, Intertek, Certified Laboratories) covering micro, heavy metals, contaminants, potency; (4) label images (front, back, Supplement Facts — real photos, not mockups).
- Resellers: purchase invoice (≤180 days, ≥400 units) + accredited lab report + label images.
- Sexual-enhancement and bodybuilding supplements require **adulterant testing to NSF/ANSI 173-2024**.
- Allowed sub-categories: beauty, fitness, vitamins/minerals/wellness, herbal, sexual wellness. **Prohibited sub-categories: weight-management supplements and "mental wellness" supplements.**

### What's banned from Shop — Prohibited Products Policy: https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=1399532709988097&lang=en **[Official]**

- "Products requiring a prescription written by a healthcare provider."
- WADA/USADA performance-enhancing substances — explicitly including **"peptide hormones,"** anabolic steroids, and SARMs. This is the clause that catches BPC-157, TB-500, ipamorelin, etc.
- "Diet pills, appetite suppressants, fat burners and creams, detox teas... any item making explicit weight loss claims."
- **"Products that use 'Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP)' or any variation of it in their branding or product title"** — an explicit GLP-1-naming ban added during the 2025 enforcement wave.
- Sexual enhancement supplements, unlicensed medicines, ingestible CBD, products glorifying eating disorders.

### Content rules for Shop sellers/affiliates — Requirements for Responsible Health-Related Content: https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=4545471832983342&lang=en **[Official]**

- No claims a product can "diagnose, treat, cure, mitigate, or prevent any disease"; no claims of "weight loss, weight gain, fat reduction, or body transformation," including "guaranteed, effortless, or permanent results"; no "Drop 10 pounds in a week."
- **Before/after photos restricted** (narrow exceptions like skin-tightening with disclaimer); no filters/effects implying results; **no weight-loss hashtags; no personal testimonials implying a product caused weight change.**
- Steer to permitted framing: "hydration, energy, recovery, balance, or confidence."
- Penalties: "content removal, listing suppression, or account-level penalties."

### 2025–2026 enforcement reality

- **Shop Safety Report (H1 2025):** 70M+ products blocked pre-listing (+40% vs prior half), 200K+ removed post-listing, 700K+ sellers removed, 1.4M seller applications rejected (https://www.tiktok.com/transparency/en/shop-safety-2025-1, https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-shop-safety-report-2025/805086/) **[Official + Journalism]**.
- **But enforcement is porous.** Media Matters, Oct 2024: TikTok Shop sellers offered semaglutide ($140/5mg vial), tirzepatide, retatrutide, BPC-157, and counterfeit fillers/Botox under "Research Use Only" labels while descriptions marketed "obesity and weight loss" (https://www.mediamatters.org/tiktok/tiktok-shop-selling-black-market-ozempic-performance-enhancement-drugs-and-cosmetic-filler). Feb 2026 follow-up: black-market GLP-1 pens returned, listed as "pen for daily writing" at $149–$319 with Affirm financing, plus Mexican tretinoin (https://www.mediamatters.org/tiktok/people-are-once-again-selling-black-market-pharmaceuticals-tiktok-shop). Sept 2025: "GLP-1 patches" (Kind Patches: 364K+ single-pack sales) thrived despite the weight-loss ban by posing as topical supplements (https://www.mediamatters.org/tiktok/unregulated-glp-1-patches-are-exploding-tiktok-shop; https://www.fastcompany.com/91402172/tiktok-is-flooded-with-glp-1-patches-despite-weight-loss-product-ban) **[Journalism]**. TikTok removed cited products after each report — enforcement is largely reactive.
- Practitioner reports of **listing "shadowbans"**: supplement listings suppressed from Shop search while affiliate links still convert; traffic collapses (1,800 to 300 daily visitors) and capped video views; multiple violations escalate to delisting/suspension/permanent ban (https://nutraseller.com/what-supplements-are-banned-or-shadowbanned-from-tiktok-shop/, https://www.blueoceanregulatory.com/blog/want-to-sell-supplements-on-tiktok-shop-watch-this-first) **[Practitioner]**.

## 4. Organic Content — Community Guidelines and the "PepTok" Phenomenon

### Community Guidelines — Regulated Goods, Services, and Commercial Activities: https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/regulated-commercial-activities **[Official]**

- TikTok "doesn't allow trading, marketing, or providing access to regulated, prohibited, or high-risk goods and services"; for the most harmful, both promotion and depiction of use are banned.
- The glossary defines **"regulated substances"** to include "illegal drugs, prescription or over-the-counter medications... and products marketed for weight loss or muscle gain." Selling/facilitating access to any of these organically = removal, and repeat violations = account ban.
- Content that merely **discusses** drugs or other regulated substances is **age-restricted (18+) and ineligible for the For You feed** — i.e., legal-but-borderline peptide talk is systematically reach-suppressed even when not removed.
- **Medical misinformation** rules separately remove "promotion of unproven, unsafe, or experimental substances or practices as medical treatments" and claims that could cause serious harm or deter proper care; unresolved claims can be made FYF-ineligible pending fact-check (https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/combating-misinformation) **[Official]**. Efficacy claims for BPC-157/retatrutide sit squarely in "unproven experimental substances."
- Weight-loss search terms/hashtags have been redirected to NEDA eating-disorder resources since 2020 (https://www.cnbc.com/2020/09/23/tiktok-bans-ads-for-fasting-apps-dieting-products.html) **[Journalism]**.

### "PepTok" — how it actually works **[Journalism/Research]**

- Infectious-disease physician Céline Gounder's May 27, 2026 investigation (https://www.celinegounder.com/p/peptides-tiktok-affiliates-misinformation): 400+ videos, 14M+ combined views. Creators post before/after results claiming "not sponsored" while carrying **affiliate discount codes**. Evasion vocabulary: **"peppers," "pep," "the pepper community," chili-pepper emojis in vendor account names**. Products carry "for research purposes only" labels yet ship with dosing instructions — "a legal fiction." Compounds: BPC-157, Melanotan II, retatrutide.
- The affiliate format is consistent: post results, tag the vendor, drop a code; sales close **off-platform via link-in-bio/linktree**, or in Telegram/Discord communities (one 35K-member Telegram group coordinates third-party testing) (https://www.peptidejournal.org/news/social-media-peptides-tiktok-reddit-influence, https://www.vectorculture.org/p/not-for-human-consumption) **[Journalism]**.
- Reconstitution tutorials for retatrutide filmed on kitchen counters, "99% purity" claims, bacteriostatic water bundles; crypto payments to gray-market peptide vendors hit $32M in Q1 2026, +159% QoQ (https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/gray-market-peptides-flood-tiktok-as-pharmacists-warn-of-safety-risks, https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/gray-market-peptide-crypto-boom/) **[Journalism]**.
- **What gets removed/banned:** accounts promoting Ozempic/Mounjaro with discount codes were banned for "violating our Community Guidelines on the trade of regulated goods and services" after a June 2025 LADbible investigation (~500K #Mounjaro posts; ~100K #Wegovy); TikTok claims 93% of violating videos are removed before any report (https://www.ladbible.com/originals/news/tiktok-instagram-ban-weight-loss-ozempic-mounjaro-896934-20250606) **[Journalism]**. TikTok's own Discover pages ("peptide accounts banned," "why are peptide sites being banned") show creator-side ban waves are common knowledge in the niche.
- **Shadowban behavior:** TikTok officially denies shadowbanning, saying it only actions repeated violations with notice (via Fox News statement); in practice the CG's FYF-ineligibility rule for regulated-substance discussion plus practitioner reports of view caps function as search/reach suppression for peptide and weight-loss terms **[Official denial + Practitioner observation]**.
- Takeaway pattern: removal is triggered mainly by **commerce signals** (codes, links, price talk, "DM to order") and **consumption instruction** (dosing, injection how-to); neutral discussion mostly gets quiet reach suppression instead.

## 5. Branded Content (Paid Partnerships / Creator Deals)

Branded Content Policy: https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/bc-policy/en; market-specific annex: https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/branded-content-policy-market-specific-requirements-archive **[Official]**

- **Mandatory disclosure:** any content where a creator receives payment or free product requires the **commercial content ("Branded Content") toggle**, which applies the paid-partnership label. Undisclosed branded content violates policy; the toggle also subjects the post to branded-content category rules.
- **Prohibited branded-content categories (globally or near-globally):**
  - "Weight loss clinics, weight loss and weight management supplements (including fat burning pills, appetite suppressants, weight loss and detox teas or lollipops) and fasting services."
  - "Illegal drugs, drug paraphernalia, recreational and prescription drugs and CBD supplements."
  - Medical institutions, treatments and devices "including nutritionists, online pharmacies and telehealth providers"; therapy/mental-health services.
  - Per the market annex, **"Food supplements and vitamins" ("Vitamins, powders, shakes, gummies claiming to have vitamin or health benefits") are prohibited as branded content in nearly all markets — including the US/Canada and EEA/UK/Switzerland lists.** A handful of markets allow vitamin supplements only as 18+-restricted branded content.
- **Practical consequence [Practitioner]:** even a fully legal multivitamin generally cannot be a *disclosed paid partnership* on TikTok in the US/EU under the letter of this policy — which is exactly why supplement brands route creator activity through **TikTok Shop affiliate content** (governed by the seller/health-content rules in §3) or run creator material as **Spark Ads through the supplement-certified ads pipeline** instead of the branded-content toggle. For peptides/GLP-1, branded content is prohibited three ways over (Rx drugs, weight-management, supplements).
- Body-image creative rules apply to branded content in both US and EU: no content that "promote[s] or reinforce[s] negative or unhealthy body images."

## 6. Enforcement Reality, Press Coverage, and the Regulatory Backdrop (2024–2026)

- **Oct 2024:** Media Matters finds black-market semaglutide/tirzepatide/retatrutide/BPC-157 and counterfeit injectables on TikTok Shop; products removed post-publication (URL in §3).
- **June 2025:** LADbible/Times reporting leads to bans of Mounjaro/Wegovy affiliate accounts; MedExpress affiliate scheme exposed (URL in §4).
- **H1 2025:** TikTok's Shop Safety "siege": 70M listings blocked, 700K sellers removed (URLs in §3).
- **Sept 2025:** "GLP-1 patch" wave — the naming ban ("GLP" in title) is circumvented by supplement-patch framing (URLs in §3).
- **Dec 2025:** UK ASA bans first influencer and public discount-code posts for weight-loss POMs; joint ASA/MHRA/GPhC enforcement program with monthly monitoring reports (URLs in §2).
- **Jan–Feb 2026:** GLP-1 pens return to Shop as "pens for daily writing"; gray-market peptide/crypto boom documented; NYT covers Silicon Valley peptide-injection culture (URLs in §3–4).
- **Mar–Apr 2026:** **FDA hits seven RUO peptide websites in one day** (Pink Pony Peptides, Mile High Compounds, Prime Sciences, Gram Peptides, PekCura Labs, FormPour, Fantasy Face) — FDA held that therapeutic marketing language ("appetite suppression, insulin sensitivity, thermogenesis") plus selling bacteriostatic water alongside vials establishes intended human drug use, **nullifying the RUO disclaimer**; 43 GLP-1/peptide-related warning letters in 2026 YTD vs 14 in all of 2024 (https://policycanary.io/blog/fda-research-peptide-warning-letters-april-2026) **[Journalism summarizing official FDA letters]**. State-level: AGs stepping into GLP-1 enforcement (https://www.stevenslee.com/health-law-observer-blog/glp-1-weight-loss-drug-enforcement-in-2025-state-attorneys-general-step-into-a-growing-regulatory-gap/); Alabama medical board warned physicians off unapproved peptides (May 2026).
- **How legitimate brands survive [Practitioner]:** compliant supplement brands succeed via ingredient-education organic content, micro-creator (10K–500K) ongoing partnerships, lifestyle framing instead of outcome claims ("show a calm morning routine" rather than "reduces stress"), and full Shop category qualification (https://soldoutbrands.com/blog/tiktok-shop-for-supplement-brands, https://socialtale.co/blog/tiktok-shop-for-supplements, https://fordebaker.com/blog/selling-supplements-on-tiktok-shop-everything-you-need-to-know-to-drive-sales-and-audience-connection-2025-guide/, https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-growth-strategies-2026).

## 7. Practical Do / Don't Lists

### A. RUO peptide companies (BPC-157, semaglutide, tirzepatide vials "for research")

**Paid ads: no path.** Do not attempt. Unapproved drugs/research chemicals are prohibited; cloaked ads risk permanent advertiser-entity bans, and post-FDA-April-2026, the ad itself is federal evidence of intended human use.

**TikTok Shop: no path.** Peptide hormones are named prohibited products; "GLP" naming is banned outright; RUO labeling is a documented enforcement trigger, not a shield.

**Organic:**
- Don't: discount codes, affiliate links, prices, "link in bio to buy," dosing/reconstitution content, before/after results, code words ("peppers" is already burned — moderation and journalists both know it).
- Survivable-but-gray: unbranded educational content about peptide science with no commerce signals — expect 18+ gating and FYF ineligibility (reach suppression) even when compliant.
- Understand the endgame: the entire PepTok affiliate model is under simultaneous platform, FDA, state-AG, and press attack. TikTok is a liability channel, not a growth channel, for this segment; anything that works is working because enforcement hasn't reached it yet.

### B. Peptide/GLP-1-adjacent wellness supplement brands (oral "GLP-1 support," etc.)

- Don't put "GLP-1," "GLP," "nature's Ozempic," semaglutide comparisons, or any weight-loss claim in product name, listing, ad, or hashtag — the naming ban is explicit and the patch precedent shows this niche is under specific journalistic surveillance.
- Don't run weight-loss positioning at all in US/EU ads or branded content (category-prohibited). Don't use before/after bodies, "appetite suppressant," "fat burner," transformation testimonials.
- Do reposition creative around permitted axes: metabolic health education, energy, satiety-adjacent lifestyle ("balanced routine"), ingredient science (berberine, chromium) without outcome promises — noting FTC substantiation still applies to implied claims.
- Do complete Shop category qualification with full documentation before listing anything.

### C. General supplement brands

- **Paid ads — do:** obtain pre-approval; upload cGMP (21 CFR 111) cert, Supplement Facts photos, facility registration, fresh lab report; target 18+; keep claims to structure/function with substantiation; keep landing pages consistent with the ad (TikTok reviews both).
- **Paid ads — don't:** disease claims, weight/fat/muscle-gain claims, "easy/guaranteed" results, body-shaming or ideal-body framing, healthcare data collection via pixel.
- **Shop — do:** real product photography of labels, accredited-lab COAs ≤180 days, invoice chain if reselling, NSF/ANSI 173-2024 adulterant testing for sexual/bodybuilding SKUs, respond to compliance inquiries within ~48 hours, monitor for listing suppression.
- **Shop — don't:** weight-management or "mental wellness" SKUs, hidden-claim hashtags, affiliate scripts with testimonials implying results.
- **Organic/creator — do:** educational/ingredient content, micro-creator ongoing relationships, routine/lifestyle framing, honest disclosure. Route paid creator work through Shop affiliate or Spark Ads rather than the branded-content toggle (supplements are a prohibited branded-content category in US/EU).
- **Organic/creator — don't:** pay creators without disclosure, brief creators with claims the brand couldn't run in an ad (the seller health-content rules apply to affiliate content and the FTC applies to everyone), or rely on virality of borderline claims — FYF ineligibility silently kills that reach.

**Cross-cutting:** document creator briefs and claim substantiation; assume UK/EU work involving any GLP-1 or weight-loss POM adjacency is regulator-monitored (ASA monthly sweep reports); and treat TikTok's pattern as consistent — permissive-looking gaps followed by press-triggered purges that take whole account portfolios with them.
