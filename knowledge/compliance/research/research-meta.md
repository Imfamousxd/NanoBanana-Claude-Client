# Meta (Facebook + Instagram) Compliance Report: Peptide, GLP-1, and Wellness-Supplement Brands
**Research date: July 22, 2026.** Covers paid ads, organic content, commerce, and branded content. Each item is tagged **[OFFICIAL]** (Meta/LegitScript primary source), **[REPORTED]** (news/legal analysis), or **[PRACTITIONER]** (agency/consultant interpretation — treat as informed opinion, not policy).

---

## 1. Policy-by-Policy Summary (Paid Ads — Meta Advertising Standards)

### 1.1 Drugs and Pharmaceuticals (Restricted Goods & Services) — the controlling policy for peptides
**URL:** https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drugs-pharmaceuticals/ **[OFFICIAL]**

- Prohibits ads that "solicit, buy, sell, trade, donate or gift... illicit, recreational, or other potentially unsafe drugs," and ads that encourage their consumption.
- Ads may not claim a product can "treat, cure, prevent, mitigate or diagnose a disease or medical condition."
- **Prescription drugs are RESTRICTED, not banned**: allowed only for pre-authorized advertisers — online pharmacies, telemedicine/telehealth providers, and pharmaceutical manufacturers. Pharmacies/telehealth need **active LegitScript certification plus written Meta authorization**; manufacturers go through Meta's internal pre-approval instead of LegitScript (structure in place since Meta's Aug 2021 policy update — see Nixon Peabody analysis: https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2021/08/05/facebook-pharma-ad-policy and National Law Review: https://natlawreview.com/article/new-facebook-policy-requires-certification-and-pre-approval-telemedicine-company **[REPORTED]**).
- Rx-drug ads: **18+ only**, targetable only in **US, Canada, New Zealand**, and must include a consult-a-licensed-health-professional disclaimer.
- OTC medicines: allowed to 18+ audiences without certification, subject to local law.
- **Semaglutide, tirzepatide, and other GLP-1s are prescription drugs** — so any ad promoting them (branded, compounded, or "research" vials) falls under this restricted path. The policy page itself never mentions "peptides," "GLP-1," or "compounded" by name; classification is functional.

### 1.2 Unsafe Substances and Supplements
**URL (as currently indexed):** https://transparency.meta.com/en-gb/policies/ad-standards/dangerous-content/unsafe-supplements **[OFFICIAL]** — note: this page 404'd on direct fetch during research (Meta has been reshuffling Transparency Center slugs); content confirmed via search index and multiple secondary summaries.

- "Ads must not promote the sale or use of illicit or recreational drugs, or other unsafe substances, products or supplements, **as determined by Meta at its sole discretion**."
- Named examples of unsafe supplements: **anabolic steroids, chitosan, comfrey, dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), ephedra, and human growth hormones**. The HGH listing is the closest explicit analog to research peptides — practitioners treat GH secretagogues (ipamorelin, CJC-1295, tesamorelin, MK-677 etc.) as falling here. **[PRACTITIONER inference from OFFICIAL list]**
- "Sole discretion" is the operative phrase: Meta does not publish a peptide list; it classifies at review time. Consumer Reports has documented this ambiguity: https://www.consumerreports.org/health/supplements/dangerous-illegal-supplements-promoted-on-facebook-a6605223059/ **[REPORTED]**
- Only exception: political advocacy, news, and awareness content referring to prohibited substances without promoting sale/consumption.

### 1.3 Health and Wellness (weight loss, supplements, cosmetic) — successor to "Personal Health and Appearance"
**URL:** https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/ **[OFFICIAL]**

The legacy "Personal Health and Appearance" policy was folded into this Restricted Goods "Health and Wellness" page plus body-image rules during Meta's 2023–2024 Transparency Center reorganization. Current rules:

- "Ads promoting or marketing dietary, health, or weight loss or weight gain products and services **must be targeted to people at least 18 years or older**." (Same for cosmetics/procedures.)
- Ads must not contain "statements of inferiority about physical appearance" or attempt to **generate negative self-perception**, and must not assert an ideal body type.
- Explicitly banned imagery: "**Close up on specific body area by pinching fat**." Before/after transformation imagery is prohibited for **weight-loss** products (permitted only for general cosmetic products/procedures at 18+).
- Banned claim structures: "sensational language with exaggerated or extreme claims, or **promises of specific outcomes within a set timeframe** without disclaimers or qualifiers" — this is the official basis for the "Lose X lbs in Y days" prohibition.
- Weight-loss ads that do run must indicate realistic timeframes for depicted results.

### 1.4 Personal Attributes
**[OFFICIAL]** (part of Advertising Standards; see intro at https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/) — ads may not imply knowledge of a user's medical condition or health status ("Struggling with your weight?", "Need semaglutide?"). Frequent rejection driver for peptide/GLP-1 funnels. Practitioner overview: https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/meta-ads-policy-unapproved-health-claims-explained **[PRACTITIONER]**

### 1.5 Misleading / Unacceptable Business Practices
**[OFFICIAL]** — unsubstantiated efficacy claims, fake testimonials, "clinically proven" without substantiation, and deceptive landing-page mismatch (cloaking) are enforced at the account level, not just ad level.

### 1.6 January 2025 "Health & Wellness" DATA restrictions (major recent change)
**[OFFICIAL behavior, announced to advertisers Dec 2024, effective Jan 2025; no single public help-center URL — documented by law firm Foley Hoag:** https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/security-privacy-and-the-law/2025/january/meta-s-new-advertising-rules-key-considerations-for-health-and-wellness-businesses/ **and agencies:** https://www.cardinaldigitalmarketing.com/healthcare-resources/blog/meta-announces-major-changes-healthcare-advertising/ , https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/meta-health-and-wellness-brands **]**

- Any business whose site/page "mentions medical conditions, treatments, or wellness services" is auto-classified as a health & wellness advertiser and placed into a restriction tier ("Core Setup" default; mid/full-restricted for more sensitive properties).
- Flagged advertisers **cannot optimize on lower-funnel events (Purchase, Add to Cart, appointment bookings)** — only Landing Page Views, Engagement, etc.; URL parameters and custom parameters stripped; retargeting/lookalike audiences built on conversion data degrade or stop working.
- Enforcement escalated through 2025–2026: Meta disabled custom audiences and custom conversions whose *names* implied health conditions (per https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/insights/guide-to-social-media-health-ad-restrictions-2026/ **[PRACTITIONER]**).
- Practical effect on peptide/supplement brands: even fully "compliant" wellness advertisers lost purchase optimization — this is a data/privacy restriction, independent of creative policy.

## 2. Flat-Out PROHIBITED on Meta (no unlock path)

| Item | Basis |
|---|---|
| Ads for **injectable research peptides / RUO vials** (BPC-157, TB-500, epithalon, "research" semaglutide/tirzepatide, etc.) | Not named in any policy, but prohibited three ways at once: unapproved drugs = "unsafe substances" (Meta's sole discretion); semaglutide/tirzepatide = prescription drugs an RUO vendor can never be authorized for; ingestible/injectable products barred from commerce surfaces. **The "not for human consumption" label provides zero protection in Meta's system — there is no ad category for research chemicals, and LegitScript (Meta's certifier) explicitly treats RUO-labeled peptides marketed to consumers as violative** (LegitScript peptide research, Dec 10, 2025, via https://ppc.land/unapproved-peptides-ads-surge-208-as-marketplaces-see-276-sales-growth/) **[OFFICIAL + REPORTED]** |
| HGH and anabolic steroids in any form | Named on the unsafe-substances list **[OFFICIAL]** |
| Disease claims for any supplement ("heals tendons," "reverses aging," "treats diabetes") | Drugs & Pharma + misleading-claims policies **[OFFICIAL]** |
| Weight-loss **before/after** imagery; pinch-the-fat closeups; "lose X lbs in Y days" | Health & Wellness policy **[OFFICIAL]** |
| Negative self-perception / body-inferiority creative | Health & Wellness + body-image rules **[OFFICIAL]** |
| Any weight-loss/supplement ad targeting under-18s | Health & Wellness policy **[OFFICIAL]** |
| Selling Rx drugs or ingestible supplements via Shops / Marketplace / product tagging | Commerce Policies #13 & #16 **[OFFICIAL]** |
| Compounded GLP-1 marketed as equivalent to Wegovy/Zepbound, or "cheap semaglutide" price-blast ads | Combination of Meta Rx rules + FDA/FTC enforcement wave (Sept 2025 onward); post-shortage, mass compounding itself is largely unlawful **[REPORTED]** — https://www.statnews.com/2025/09/19/fda-obesity-semaglutide-tirzepatide-telehealth-warning-compounding-glp1/ , https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-warns-30-telehealth-companies-against-illegal-marketing-compounded-glp-1s |

## 3. RESTRICTED — and the unlock path

### 3.1 Prescription drugs / telehealth / online pharmacy (the only legit GLP-1 ad path)
1. **Get LegitScript Healthcare Merchant or Telemedicine certification** — https://www.legitscript.com/certification/healthcare-certification/ , https://www.legitscript.com/certification/telemedicine/ **[OFFICIAL]**
   - **Who can certify:** licensed pharmacies, telehealth platforms with real practitioner oversight, and (for weight loss) providers operating within FDA compounding rules. A brand selling RUO vials or non-prescription "GLP-1 support" supplements **cannot** get pharmacy/telemedicine certification.
   - **Cost:** ~$975 application + ~$2,150/yr per website for healthcare merchants (2025–2026 fee structure); probationary pricing higher. **Timeline:** typically 4–8 weeks with documentation ready. (Karpa Health 2026 guide: https://karpahealth.com/resources/legitscript-certification-telehealth-guide/ **[PRACTITIONER, fee figures corroborated by LegitScript FAQ]** https://www.legitscript.com/certification/healthcare-certification/faq/)
   - **What it unlocks:** Rx-drug ads on Meta (plus Google, Microsoft, TikTok, LinkedIn) and increasingly payment processing (Visa VIRP / Mastercard registration). LegitScript on weight-loss meds: https://www.legitscript.com/healthcare/navigating-legitscript-certification-for-weight-loss-medications-common-inquiries-and-answers/ **[OFFICIAL]**
2. **Apply to Meta for written authorization** (form in Business Help Center; Meta also runs an internal review alternative for some advertisers, and a separate pre-approval track for pharma manufacturers).
3. **Then**: US/CA/NZ targeting only, 18+, consult-a-professional disclaimer, no efficacy overclaim. Compounded GLP-1 post-shortage: 503A compounding of tirzepatide had to stop by March 5, 2025 and semaglutide by April 24, 2025 (FDA shortage resolution) — certified telehealth advertisers now market **the medical program/consultation**, not the compounded drug ("From Shortage Heroes to Compliance Champions": https://www.legitscript.com/healthcare/from-shortage-heroes-to-compliance-champions-what-todays-glp-1-pharmacies-must-know/ **[OFFICIAL]**). Hims & Hers' $49 compounded-oral-semaglutide gambit collapsed within days after FDA called such products "illegal copycats"; Novo Nordisk sued Hims Feb 9, 2026 **[REPORTED]**.

### 3.2 General wellness supplements (no drug claims)
- **Allowed without certification** if: 18+ targeting, structure/function-style claims only, no unsafe ingredients, no before/after or negative self-perception creative. Some practitioner sources report Meta now expects the FDA structure/function disclaimer in ad copy for supplement ads **[PRACTITIONER — not verified in official policy text]** (https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-health-wellness-restricted-ads-2026-supplements-body-image-medical-claim-rules).
- Expect the Jan 2025 data-restriction tier regardless of creative compliance.

## 4. ORGANIC Content Rules (can a peptide brand even run an Instagram page?)

### 4.1 Community Standards — Restricted Goods and Services
**URL:** https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/restricted-goods-services/ **[OFFICIAL]** (page is client-rendered; policy language below confirmed via Meta's Oversight Board ketamine case page https://transparency.meta.com/oversight/oversight-board-cases/ketamine-medical-treatment and Meta's organic-content policy paper https://about.fb.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/PPF_Promotion-of-Regulated-Goods-in-Organic-Content_09.16.2020.pdf)

- **Pharmaceutical drugs** = "drugs that require a prescription or medical professionals to administer." Organic content may not **attempt to buy, sell, trade, co-ordinate the trade of, donate, gift or ask for** pharmaceutical drugs. This applies to private individuals, manufacturers, AND retailers; the carve-outs are for legitimate healthcare context (pharmacy delivery services by real healthcare businesses, education, news, advocacy).
- **Non-medical drugs** (used to get high / outside medical purpose) are treated more strictly — even admissions and promotion of use are restricted.
- **Discussion is allowed**: educational content about medications, personal medical-treatment experiences, policy debate, recovery content. The Oversight Board ketamine case confirmed Meta permits "broader discussion" of medically administered pharmaceuticals.
- **So: yes, a peptide brand can operate an Instagram/Facebook page** — as brand-awareness and education. What converts a lawful page into a removable one is **sales facilitation signals**: product + price + a way to obtain it (link in bio to a checkout for Rx-class compounds, "DM to order," discount codes, "link in bio 💉"). Meta states it removes buy/sell pharmaceutical content in organic posts wherever found (enforcement data: https://transparency.meta.com/reports/community-standards-enforcement/regulated-goods/ **[OFFICIAL]**). RUO semaglutide/tirzepatide/retatrutide posts are pharmaceutical-drug sales content regardless of the research disclaimer.
- Repeated violations escalate to page/profile removal under **Account Integrity**: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/account-integrity/ and https://transparency.meta.com/enforcement/taking-action/restricting-accounts/ **[OFFICIAL]**.
- Enforcement is demonstrably leaky — LegitScript measured a **75% YoY increase (2023→2024) in social-media peptide sales content** and CNN/The Conversation documented flourishing influencer peptide promotion through 2025–2026 (https://theconversation.com/influencers-are-promoting-dangerous-peptides-on-social-media-and-regulators-are-struggling-to-keep-up-282905 **[REPORTED]**). Leaky ≠ permitted: takedowns are batchy and retroactive.

### 4.2 Commerce Policies (Shops, Marketplace, Instagram product tagging)
**URL:** https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce **[OFFICIAL]** — verbatim:
- **#13 Medical and Healthcare Products:** "Commerce content may not promote the buying, selling, or trading of medical and healthcare products and services, including medical devices or **ingestible supplements**."
- **#16 Prescription Products, Drugs and Drug Paraphernalia:** "Commerce content may not promote the buying, selling, or trading of drugs, drug paraphernalia or prescription products."
- **Net effect: no Instagram Shopping / product tagging for peptides OR ordinary ingestible supplements.** Even a fully compliant vitamin brand cannot tag ingestible products. Topical/non-ingestible items are outside this exclusion.

### 4.3 Branded Content / Influencer Rules
**URLs:** https://www.facebook.com/policies/brandedcontent (Meta), https://help.instagram.com/1695974997209192 (Instagram), Business Help Centre copy: https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/221149188908254 **[OFFICIAL — pages are login-gated/JS-rendered; provisions below corroborated via multiple secondary summaries]**

- Any content featuring/influenced by a business partner for value **must use the Paid Partnership label**.
- Branded content **cannot promote prohibited categories at all** — including illegal/prescription/recreational drugs and **unsafe supplements**. An influencer post pushing BPC-157 for a fee violates branded-content policy per se (precedent: Instagram's 2019 branded-content ban on vaping/weapons — https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/social-media/instagram-bans-influencers-getting-paid-promote-vaping-guns-n1104531 **[REPORTED]**).
- Restricted categories flow through: online pharmacies require the **business partner** to hold LegitScript certification + written Meta permission; supplement/weight-loss branded content must be age-gated 18+; boosted branded content must additionally satisfy all Advertising Standards.
- Reality check: the influencer-peptide economy (discount codes, affiliate commissions, testimonial reels) operates almost entirely outside these rules and is the FTC's and FDA's current enforcement focus as much as Meta's **[REPORTED]**.

## 5. Enforcement Reality (what actually happens)

**The ladder** **[OFFICIAL framework + PRACTITIONER detail]:**
1. **Ad rejection** — most common; automated at submission; ~30% initial rejection rates reported for supplement ads (https://www.flighted.co/blog/meta-ads-strategy-for-supplement-brands-2026).
2. **Account restriction** — spend caps, forced manual review, feature loss. Meta states restrictions are driven by *cumulative advertiser behavior scoring*, not single ads ("we monitor and investigate advertiser behavior": https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/ ; https://transparency.meta.com/enforcement/taking-action/restricting-accounts/).
3. **Ad account disable** — for deceptive/dangerous-harm violations or persistence.
4. **Business Manager / business portfolio disable** — nukes all pages, pixels, ad accounts, and often flags associated admins' personal profiles, domains, and payment methods. Circumvention attempts (new BMs, borrowed accounts) feed the "Circumventing Systems" policy and produce faster re-bans.
5. Recovery: appeals via Account Quality; practitioners describe "Recovery Action Plans" with compliance training + probation for some advertisers **[PRACTITIONER]** (https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-disabled-ad-account-recovery-2026-account-quality-appeals-review-path).

**Documented peptide-vertical cases:**
- Orange Trail case study (agency selling access to "enterprise agency ad accounts"): peptide brand had **two full account suspensions in six months**, >40% disapproval rates, and a ~$900/day spend ceiling before restructuring; scaled to $150k+/mo after creative/landing sanitization — https://orangetrail.io/case/scaling-peptides-without-shutdowns-from-account-suspension-stable-monthly-spend/ **[PRACTITIONER — and note: renting "agency ad accounts with established trust histories" to a policy-prohibited vertical is itself circumvention of Meta's enforcement, i.e., ban-on-discovery territory, not compliance]**.
- LegitScript (Dec 10, 2025): unapproved peptide **ads up 208% (2023–2024), 678% (2022–2024)**; marketplace sales up 276% over five years; names Melanotan, BPC-157, TB-500, PT-141, GLP-1 peptides, Ipamorelin, Tesamorelin, CJC-1295 — https://ppc.land/unapproved-peptides-ads-surge-208-as-marketplaces-see-276-sales-growth/ **[REPORTED]**. Translation: lots of RUO ads run on Meta *until* they're swept — volume proves enforcement lag, not permission.
- Off-platform risk went criminal in 2025: FDA warning letters to RUO peptide sellers (Dec 2024 wave incl. retatrutide/semaglutide/tirzepatide "research" vials; 50+ letters in a single month of 2025), the June 2025 Amino Asylum raid, DOJ guilty pleas from gray-market distributors, and Peptide Sciences' voluntary shutdown — https://rethinkpeptides.com/articles/for-research-use-only-the-legal-fiction-of-gray-market-peptides , https://thepeptidecatalog.com/articles/peptide-sciences-shut-down-alternatives **[REPORTED]**.

**"Compliant funnel" patterns — what's actually within policy vs gray-area:**

| Tactic | Status |
|---|---|
| Educational/ingredient-science lead magnets with no product sale in ad or landing page; retarget email list off-platform | Within policy **if** the funnel's product is itself advertisable; for RUO peptides the *destination* still sells a prohibited product — Meta reviews landing pages and whole domains, so this is **gray-to-violating** |
| "Trust/quality" creative (lab testing, sourcing, COAs) with no health claims, for a wellness supplement | Within policy **[PRACTITIONER consensus]** |
| Same "lab-grade purity" framing for RUO injectables ("for lab use, not lifestyle use") | **Gray-area at best** — linguistic substitution doesn't change product classification; LegitScript-style review looks at intended consumer use (per the Medium/YourPeptideBrand playbook itself: https://medium.com/@yourpeptidebrand/how-to-advertise-peptides-without-getting-banned-a-compliance-first-strategy-for-research-only-70ed897a39bc **[PRACTITIONER]**) |
| Separate "clean" ad domain vs real sales site (cloaking) | **Violation** — Circumventing Systems; classic BM-ban trigger |
| Rented/agency ad accounts to outlive bans | **Violation** of Meta ToS; escalates to business-level bans |
| Telehealth GLP-1: certified entity advertises the medical program, doctor consult, and lifestyle support without naming price-per-vial or promising equivalence to branded drugs | Within policy via the LegitScript + authorization path **[OFFICIAL]** |

## 6. Practical Do / Don't for Peptide-Adjacent Brands on Meta

**DO**
- Segment the portfolio: (a) RUO vial brands — treat Meta paid as **unavailable**; organic limited to unbranded education with no purchase path; (b) telehealth/GLP-1 — pursue LegitScript ($975 + $2,150/yr, 4–8 wks) then Meta authorization; (c) wellness supplements — advertise under Health & Wellness rules.
- Target 18+ on everything health-related; US/CA/NZ only for anything Rx.
- Use structure/function language ("supports," "promotes") with substantiation on file; keep landing page claims identical in compliance level to ad claims — Meta scores the whole funnel.
- Expect and plan around the Jan 2025 data restrictions: optimize to Landing Page Views/engagement, build first-party lists, move conversion measurement server-side within Meta's allowed events.
- Keep organic pages educational: science explainers, brand story, community. Keep required disclosures legible in the asset itself.
- Use Paid Partnership labels on every gifted/paid creator post; vet that the promoted product category is even branded-content-eligible first.

**DON'T**
- Don't run ads (or boosted posts, which are ads) for injectable/RUO peptides under any wording — "research use only" is not a recognized Meta category and reads to reviewers as evasion.
- Don't post price + product + acquisition path (link, DM, code) for Rx-class compounds organically; don't run "DM to order" or discount-code influencer schemes.
- Don't use before/after, pinched fat, scale/tape-measure closeups, "lose X lbs in Y days," or "feel bad → buy fix" creative anywhere in weight-loss adjacent work.
- Don't imply the viewer's health status ("struggling with your weight?") — Personal Attributes violation.
- Don't tag ingestible products in Shops/Instagram Shopping — categorically excluded.
- Don't buy/rent aged or agency ad accounts, clone BMs after a ban, or cloak landing pages — these convert an ad-level problem into a business-level and potentially permanent one.
- Don't market compounded GLP-1s as available/equivalent/cheap post-shortage; the FDA (30+ warning letters, Sept 2025 onward), FTC, and Novo/Lilly litigation are all active in that lane, independent of Meta.

**Key open items / verification notes:** Meta's Transparency Center URLs are unstable mid-2026 (the Unsafe Substances page and Community Standards Restricted Goods page resist direct fetching; content herein was cross-confirmed via Meta's Oversight Board case pages, Meta's 2020 organic-content policy PDF, and current search-index snapshots). No public Meta statement specifically naming "peptides" was found — all peptide treatment is by classification under existing categories. The supplement-disclaimer-in-ad-copy requirement and "Recovery Action Plan" mechanics are practitioner-reported and should be validated against the advertiser's own Account Quality surface before relying on them.
