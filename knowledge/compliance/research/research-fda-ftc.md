# US Regulatory Layer Governing Peptide & Supplement Marketing — Agency Research Brief (July 2026)

**Scope:** FDA + FTC + state law as applied to *marketing content* (paid ads, organic social, influencer, email, web) for (a) research-use-only (RUO) peptide sellers, (b) peptide/GLP-1 wellness/supplement brands, (c) general supplement brands.

**How to read the authority tags:**
- **[LAW]** = binding statute or regulation (FDCA, FTC Act, CFR, a final Rule).
- **[GUIDANCE]** = agency guidance / interpretation (persuasive, not independently binding, but tells you how the agency reads the law).
- **[ENFORCEMENT]** = pattern from warning letters, consent orders, DOJ actions, NAD decisions (shows what actually gets punished; warning letters are not law but are the clearest signal of agency priorities).

## 1. FDA STATUS OF COMMONLY MARKETED PEPTIDES

### 1a. The three-bucket framework

**(i) FDA-approved drugs/biologics** — legal to prescribe; illegal to sell without a prescription; cannot be sold as "research chemicals" for human use; cannot be in a supplement.
- **Semaglutide** (Ozempic/Wegovy/Rybelsus – Novo Nordisk) and **tirzepatide** (Mounjaro/Zepbound – Eli Lilly): approved drugs. **[LAW]**
- **Tesamorelin** (Egrifta/Egrifta SV/Egrifta WR): the only FDA-approved GHRH-analog peptide, approved 2010 for HIV lipodystrophy. (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12791536/, https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2019/022505Orig1s010lbl.pdf)
- **Sermorelin**: originally approved as a GH-deficiency diagnostic; original brand discontinued; now compounded but holds no active approval for anti-aging/GH indications. (https://peptidesexplorer.com/blog/is-tesamorelin-fda-approved)

**(ii) Not approved; on/under review for the compounding "bulk substances" lists** — see 1b.

**(iii) Not FDA-approved for anything** — BPC-157, TB-500/TB-4, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, epithalon/epitalon, GHK-Cu, AOD-9604, Semax, Dihexa, Melanotan II, KPV, MOTS-c, DSIP, retatrutide, cagrilintide, mazdutide (the last three are investigational drug candidates, not approved). (https://www.optmzd.app/blog/fda-approved-peptides)

### 1b. The 503A/503B compounding "bulk drug substances" list — Category 2 "significant safety risks" **[LAW/regulatory action]**
FDA's official Category 2 list: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks

- **Sept 2023:** FDA moved a batch of peptides into **Category 2** of the interim §503A bulks list, citing safety risks (immunogenicity, thin human data). Once in Category 2, licensed **503A compounding pharmacies cannot legally compound the substance for humans.** Peptides affected included **BPC-157, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, and others.** (https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=2e55b76a-3173-4e04-beda-bf021202f18d, https://www.restorehealthconsulting.com/news/fda-adds-several-peptides-to-category-2-bulks-list-restricting-them-from-compounding)
- **Critical nuance:** these peptides were **never in Category 1** — meaning they were never validly compoundable to begin with. (https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/04/fdas-peptide-rally-what-compounders-and-industry-need-to-know-post-1-of-2/)
- **Sept 2024:** FDA **removed five** peptide substances (incl. **CJC-1295 and ipamorelin acetate**) from Category 2 — but only because nominators *withdrew the nominations*, not a safety clearance. Removal ≠ approval; they remain unapproved and not on Category 1.
- **BPC-157** specifically remains flagged for significant safety risk. (https://www.opss.org/article/bpc-157-prohibited-peptide-and-unapproved-drug-found-health-and-wellness-products)
- **Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) meeting July 23–24, 2026** (this month) is reviewing seven peptides for potential §503A inclusion: **July 23** — BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c; **July 24** — DSIP (emideltide), Semax, Epitalon. A further tranche (GHK-Cu, Melanotan II, LL-37/cathelicidin, Dihexa, PEG-MGF) is slated for ~Feb 2027. Even a favorable PCAC vote requires notice-and-comment rulemaking (>1 year). (https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/04/fdas-peptide-rally-what-compounders-and-industry-need-to-know-post-1-of-2/)

**Marketing takeaway:** For RUO sellers and med-spa/compounding clients, there is **no lawful "compounded for humans" path** for BPC-157, TB-500, epithalon, Semax, KPV, MOTS-c, etc. today. Content implying a clinic can legally provide these for human use is high-risk.

### 1c. Excluded from the dietary-supplement definition (the "drug preclusion" clause) **[LAW — FDCA §201(ff)(3)(B), 21 U.S.C. §321(ff)(3)(B)]**
A substance is **excluded from being a dietary supplement/ingredient** if it was first approved as a drug **or** was first authorized for investigation as a new drug (an IND that went into effect and was made public) with substantial clinical investigations instituted — *unless* it was marketed as a supplement/food **before** that drug investigation. (https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2024/09/the-national-products-association-challenges-fdas-interpretation-of-the-dietary-supplement-exclusionary-clause/, https://www.nutraceuticalsworld.com/understanding-preclusion-provisions-when-is-an-article-excluded-from-use-as-a-dietary-ingredient/)
- **Consequence:** **Semaglutide, tirzepatide, and essentially all drug-candidate peptides are excluded** — they cannot lawfully be sold as, or in, a "dietary supplement." Calling a peptide vial a "supplement" does not make it one.
- **NMN reversal (contrast case):** FDA had excluded **NMN** (a NAD+ precursor) under drug preclusion in 2022, then **reversed in Sept 2025**, declaring NMN lawful in supplements because it had been marketed as a supplement (~2017) before the drug investigation. The exclusion is fact-specific and the "marketed-first" carve-out matters. (https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/09/30/fda-declares-nmn-lawful-in-dietary-supplements/, https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/12/09/fda-reinstates-ndi-status-of-nmn-with-new-letters-to-ingredient-players/). NAD+/NMN can be legitimate supplement ingredients; the peptide drug candidates cannot.

### 1d. End of the GLP-1 shortage & the 2025–2026 crackdown on compounded GLP-1 marketing **[LAW + ENFORCEMENT]**
- **Shortage resolved:** tirzepatide **Dec 19, 2024**; semaglutide **Feb 21, 2025**. Enforcement-discretion windows for mass compounding **ended June 17, 2025**. (https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fda-clarifies-policies-compounders-national-glp-1-supply-begins-stabilize, https://www.hchlawyers.com/blog/2025/march/fda-update-current-guidelines-for-semaglutide-an/)
- **Manufacturer offensive:** Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk sent cease-and-desist letters to telehealth companies, wellness centers, and med-spas, then filed suit; **Novo Nordisk sued 12 defendants including pharmacies on Aug 4, 2025.** (https://www.bipc.com/major-update-on-glp-1-litigation-involving-compounding-pharmacies)
- **FDA marketing enforcement:** **Sept 2025** – 50+ warning letters to GLP-1 compounders/manufacturers, plus a letter to **Hims & Hers CEO** over claims that compounded semaglutide is "the same as" Ozempic/Wegovy. **March 3, 2026** – **30 warning letters in one day** to telehealth companies. (https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/fda-sends-warning-letters-to-more-than-50-glp-1-compounders-and-manufacturers.html, https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/03/fdas-latest-glp-1-crackdown-what-compounders)
- **Two claim types FDA is hammering:** (1) **equivalence claims** — presenting a compounded GLP-1 as "generic," "the same active ingredient," or producing "the same clinical results" as the approved drug is **false/misleading**; (2) **source-obscuring branding** — private-label presentation hiding which entity actually compounds it. (https://www.ajmc.com/view/fda-to-restrict-ingredients-used-in-mass-marketed-compounded-glp-1s-crack-down-on-misleading-ads)

## 2. THE "RESEARCH USE ONLY" FICTION — INTENDED-USE DOCTRINE & ENFORCEMENT

### 2a. The legal engine: "intended use" **[LAW — 21 CFR 201.128; FDCA §201(g)]**
A product's regulatory identity turns on the seller's **objective intent**, shown by "expressions… the design or composition of the article, or by the circumstances surrounding distribution," including labeling claims, **advertising, and oral or written statements** by the seller or its representatives, and circumstances where the article is *offered or used* for a purpose for which it is neither labeled nor advertised. (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-201/subpart-D/section-201.128)
- An "RUO / not for human consumption" label is **just one data point.** FDA weighs the **total marketing context** and can override the disclaimer.
- FDA has cited companies for **"liking" third-party testimonials on social media**, treating the like as adopting a claim that provides "evidence of intended use in the form of personal testimonials recommending or describing use for the cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease." (https://arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/publications/2020/02/all-eyes-on-influencers, https://www.tuckerellis.com/alerts/fda-warning-letters-target-internet-testimonials/) — i.e., **reposts, likes, and shares of human-result testimonials are intended-use evidence.**

### 2b. What FDA actually said in RUO peptide warning letters **[ENFORCEMENT]**
**April 2026 — seven RUO peptide sellers hit in a single day** (all letters dated **March 31, 2026**, from CDER): **Prime Sciences, Lovega (dba Pink Pony Peptides), Gram Peptides, FormPour, Guangzhou Huli Technology (dba Fantasy Face), Mile High Compounds, PekCura Labs.** (https://policycanary.io/blog/fda-research-peptide-warning-letters-april-2026, https://www.healthlawalliance.com/blog/fda-targets-glp-1-and-peptide-compounding-advertising-and-research-use-only-labeling, https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026)
- FDA's verbatim reasoning pattern: *"evidence obtained from your website establishes that your products are intended to be drugs for human use"*; and *"Despite statements on your product labeling marketing your products for 'laboratory research purposes only'… your products are drugs as defined by section 201(g)(1)."*
- The disclaimers "didn't matter" because **product pages described appetite suppression, weight loss, and glucose regulation.**
- **Selling bacteriostatic water / injection supplies alongside the vials** was cited as establishing **combination human-use intent** (Prime Sciences, Lovega, Gram Peptides). Bundling reconstitution or injection accessories = human-use evidence.
- Products: mostly semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide, plus BPC-157.

**Dec 10, 2024 — Prime Peptides (Prime Vitality), Summit Research Peptides, Swisschems, Xcel Research** (https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-roundup-december-17-2024, https://www.biospace.com/fda/fda-issues-warning-letters-against-glp-1-sellers)
- FDA reviewed both **websites and social media (Facebook, Instagram, YouTube)** and found products positioned *"to prevent, treat, or cure disease conditions and/or affect the structure or function of the body."*
- Cited example: **Summit's Facebook post** titled *"Enhanced Weight Loss: Achieve superior weight reduction with the synergistic effects of Cagrilintide and Semaglutide."*
- FDA's posture: it is **"no longer tolerating the 'for research use only' disclaimer as a shield."**

**Feb 26, 2025 — USAPeptide.com** (https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/usapeptidecom-696885-02262025); **Dec 12, 2025 — Pinnacle Professional Research dba Pinnacle Peptides** (https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/pinnacle-professional-research-dba-pinnacle-peptides-719337-12122025). Aggregate: **50+ warning letters to peptide/GLP-1 vendors by Sept 2025.** Trend per memory + TikTok agent corroboration: 14 letters (2024) → 41–43 (early/YTD 2026).

### 2c. DOJ / criminal actions **[ENFORCEMENT]**
- **U.S. v. Justin Bradley Watkins (D. Utah, 2025):** osteopathic physician indicted for receiving **misbranded drugs from China** (Feb 2024–Apr 2025) — tirzepatide, semaglutide, retatrutide, cagrilintide, **BPC-157, TB-500, ipamorelin, CJC-1295, GHK, GHK-Cu, NAD+** — relabeling vials and supplying clinic staff. (https://www.justice.gov/usao-ut/pr/utah-licensed-osteopathic-physician-indicted-allegedly-receiving-misbranded-drugs-china, https://www.stevenslee.com/health-law-observer-blog/indictment-of-osteopathic-physician-signals-shift-in-peptide-enforcement/)
- **Tailor Made Compounding LLC:** DOJ action over unapproved peptides incl. BPC-157; **$1.79M forfeiture.** (https://peptidelaws.com/news/recent-doj-actions-against-illegal-peptide-distributors)
- Reported federal "Operation" targeting interstate unapproved-peptide distribution with multiple indictments. (https://www.amcdefenselaw.com/justice-watch/peptide-industry-federal-criminal-charges-2026)

### 2d. What converts an RUO product into an unapproved new drug (checklist derived from the letters)
Any **one** of these can flip the switch: disease/condition claims (weight loss, injury/tendon repair, glucose control, anti-aging); **dosing, titration, or reconstitution instructions**; before/after or human-result **testimonials** (including **liking/reposting** third-party ones); human imagery/lifestyle context implying self-administration; bundling **injection supplies/bac water**; "how to run a cycle/protocol" content; influencer content describing personal use; FAQs answering human-use questions. The RUO label does not cure any of these.

## 3. FTC LAYER

### 3a. Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022) **[GUIDANCE, interpreting FTC Act §5 — LAW]**
Replaced the 1998 "Dietary Supplements: An Advertising Guide." Covers **all** health-related products. Core standard for any objective health/safety claim: **"competent and reliable scientific evidence,"** now generally meaning **randomized, controlled human clinical trials (RCTs)** — for disease-type claims FTC expects **at least one well-controlled human clinical trial.** (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance, https://www.mwe.com/insights/after-almost-25-years-ftc-issues-new-health-products-compliance-guidance/, https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2023/01/ftc-issues-new-guidance-on-health-related-claims-to-replace-the-dietary-supplements-advertising-guide)
- Substantiation must exist **before** the claim is disseminated; the DSHEA disclaimer does **not** lower the substantiation bar.

### 3b. Notice of Penalty Offenses — ~670 companies (April 13, 2023) **[ENFORCEMENT mechanism activating LAW — FTC Act §5(m)(1)(B)]**
FTC sent Notices to **~670** marketers of OTC drugs, homeopathics, supplements, and functional foods. A company with **actual knowledge** that conduct is unlawful can be hit with **civil penalties up to $50,120 per violation** (now **$53,088**, inflation-adjusted). (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/04/ftc-warns-almost-700-marketing-companies-they-could-face-civil-penalties-if-they-cant-back-their, https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2023/04/ftc-warns-nearly-700-health-product-marketers-to-avoid-unsubstantiated-claims)

### 3c. Revised Endorsement Guides (effective July 26, 2023) **[GUIDANCE, interpreting §5]**
(https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/federal-trade-commission-announces-updated-advertising-guides-combat-deceptive-reviews-endorsements, https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/07/26/2023-14795/guides-concerning-the-use-of-endorsements-and-testimonials-in-advertising, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)
- **"Clear and conspicuous" now means "unavoidable."** Buried hashtags, disclosures below the fold, or relying on a platform's built-in "paid partnership" tool are **not sufficient.**
- **Platform-specific:** in video, disclosure must be in the video itself (not just description); in a livestream, repeated/on-screen throughout; in a picture-only Story/post, superimposed on the image.
- **Tagging a brand** you have a relationship with **is an endorsement** requiring disclosure.
- **Brand liability:** advertisers are liable for **influencers' unsubstantiated claims** and for failures to disclose; intermediaries (agencies!) can be liable too.
- **Enforcement follow-through:** **Nov 13, 2023** – FTC warned the **American Beverage Association, Canadian Sugar Institute, and 12 dietitian/health influencers** over Instagram/TikTok posts with inadequate paid-connection disclosures; each letter attached the Notice of Penalty Offenses. (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/11/ftc-warns-two-trade-associations-dozen-influencers-about-social-media-posts-promoting-consumption)

### 3d. Consumer Reviews & Testimonials Rule ("Fake Review Rule") — final Aug 2024, effective **Oct 21, 2024** **[LAW — 16 CFR Part 465]**
First-time-violation **civil-penalty authority**; currently **$51,744 per violation.** Prohibits: fake/AI-generated or non-user reviews; **buying or selling** reviews/testimonials; **incentivized reviews conditioned on a particular sentiment**; undisclosed **insider** reviews (employees/relatives); review **suppression**; and **buying/selling fake social-media indicators** (bot followers/views). (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)

### 3e. FTC as a GLP-1 enforcer — NextMed **[ENFORCEMENT]**
FTC final order (**Dec 3, 2025**; complaint **July 2025**) against telehealth firm **NextMed** for: unsubstantiated weight-loss claims; **fake reviews**; **before/after photos and testimonials from people who were not clients and hadn't used the drugs**; hidden subscription costs; unlawful auto-renewal/cancellation practices. **$150,000** refunds + conduct injunction. (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-approves-final-order-against-telehealth-provider-nextmed-over-charges-it-used-deceptive, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/07/ftc-takes-action-against-telemedicine-firm-nextmed-over-charges-it-used-misleading-prices-fake, https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/ftc-uses-its-consumer-protection-powers-to-regulate-sellers-of-glp-1s)

## 4. SUPPLEMENT CLAIM RULES (structure/function vs disease)

### 4a. The core divide **[LAW — FDCA §403(r)(6); 21 CFR 101.93]**
- **Structure/function (S/F) claims** describe how an ingredient affects the **normal structure/function** of the body ("supports healthy metabolism"). Allowed for supplements **without pre-approval**, but require: (1) substantiation; (2) FDA notification within **30 days** of marketing; (3) the **DSHEA disclaimer**. (https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/structurefunction-claims, https://www.chpa.org/news/2021/11/understanding-dietary-supplement-claims)
- **Disease claims** — diagnose, cure, mitigate, treat, or prevent a **disease** — are **drug claims**, **prohibited** on a supplement. **Obesity is classified as a disease**, which is why weight-loss framing is dangerous. (https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48623, https://www.supplysidesj.com/weight-management/fda-ftc-and-weight-loss-claims-part-1-of-2-)

### 4b. Required DSHEA disclaimer (and its limits) **[LAW]**
Verbatim: *"This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."*
- **Limit:** the disclaimer does **not** cure an actual disease claim, does **not** lower FTC's substantiation bar, and does not rescue implied disease claims via imagery, product name, metatags, or testimonials.

### 4c. Phrases that trip "disease claim" vs safe S/F wording
- **Prohibited (disease):** "treats obesity," "clinically proven weight loss," "burns fat to reduce BMI," "heals/repairs [injury/tendon/gut]," "reverses aging," "lowers blood sugar/treats diabetes," "reduces inflammation [as disease]," "cures," "detoxifies [disease]." (https://trytruli.com/blog/weight-loss-claims-supplements-fda-ftc-rules, https://qualitysmartsolutions.com/blog/weight-loss-supplements-and-fda-rules-what-every-brand-needs-to-market/)
- **Safer S/F:** "supports healthy metabolism," "helps maintain a healthy weight as part of a balanced diet," "supports normal appetite," "promotes healthy skin," "supports recovery after exercise." **"Weight management/maintenance" is materially safer than "weight loss."**

### 4d. NAD+ / NMN
NAD+/NMN precursors are legitimate supplement ingredients (post-Sept-2025 NMN reversal), but claims still must be S/F and substantiated. NAD+ *product* claims have drawn NAD (advertising-body) scrutiny (e.g., Niagen Bioscience matters). (https://manufacturingchemist.com/nad-supplement-claims-under-scrutiny-niagen-bioscience-advertising)

### 4e. GLP-1-adjacent supplement claims — the hottest zone **[ENFORCEMENT: NAD + class actions]**
- **Lemme GLP-1 Daily (Kourtney Kardashian):** two class actions (*Robins v. Lemme*, CA Superior Court + SDNY) challenge "Support GLP-1 / promote your body's GLP-1 production… reduce hunger & cravings, promote fat reduction" and the **"clinically studied ingredients" establishment claim.** Theory: Eriomin raised GLP-1 only ~17% with no proven weight/BMI/waist effect, and the **finished formula was never clinically tested.** Establishment ("clinically proven") claims are the crack that lets private plaintiffs challenge substantiation. (https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/04/30/lemme-lawsuits-challenge-glp-1-supplement-claims/, https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/two-class-actions-take-a-shot-at-substantiation-for-lemmes-glp-1-daily-supplement-2, https://natlawreview.com/article/two-class-actions-target-supplement-promoted-increasing-glp-1)
- **"Nature's Ozempic" / berberine framing:** flagged by FTC/NAD/FDA scrutiny in 2025; comparing a supplement to a prescription GLP-1 drug invites both deceptive-comparison and implied-disease-claim liability. (https://www.news-medical.net/health/Is-Berberine-Really-e2809cNaturee28099s-Ozempice2809d-What-Science-Says.aspx)

### 4f. NAD (National Advertising Division) compounded-GLP-1 docket 2025 **[ENFORCEMENT — self-regulatory]**
Lilly and Novo Nordisk use NAD aggressively; NAD's throughline: **studies of the *approved* drug do NOT substantiate claims for a compounded product** that differs in API, formulation, or route.
- **Willow Health Services** — Report **#7488** (Dec 4, 2025), challenger Novo Nordisk; Willow refused to comply → **NAD referred it to state AGs, FTC, and ad platforms.** (https://bbbprograms.org/media/newsroom/decisions/willow-health)
- Voluntary-discontinuance companions: **Regen Doctors (#7516), Fletcher Family Medical Center / Subsema (#7518), Striker Pharmacy (#7446), Begin Anew MedSpa (#7404), Hormone Fitness (#7447)** — all compounded semaglutide/tirzepatide+B12. (https://natlawreview.com/article/national-advertising-division-puts-compounded-glp-1-advertising-diet, https://bbbprograms.org/media/newsroom/decisions/regen-doctors)

## 5. STATE-LEVEL

State AGs are the **front line** on GLP-1/peptide marketing, using consumer-protection/UDAP statutes (broader and faster than FDA process). (https://www.stevenslee.com/health-law-observer-blog/glp-1-weight-loss-drug-enforcement-in-2025-state-attorneys-general-step-into-a-growing-regulatory-gap/, https://natlawreview.com/article/federal-and-state-agencies-ramp-scrutiny-glp-1-drug-market)
- **Connecticut (AG Tong)** — settled with **Triggered Brand** (sold raw semaglutide/tirzepatide powder to consumers): **$300,000 judgment, suspended after $18,500**, under **CUTPA**. Cease-and-desist to **CT Weight Loss Shot, Vigorous 360 Wellness, Radiance Medspa** for falsely claiming compounded shots were **"FDA approved" / "compounded versions of Ozempic and Mounjaro"**; also required non-clinician owners to divest (corporate-practice-of-medicine angle). (https://portal.ct.gov/ag/press-releases/2025-press-releases/attorney-general-tong-announces-new-developments-in-crackdown-on-bootleg-glp-1-weight-loss-drugs)
- **Mississippi (AG Fitch)** — consumer alert against unapproved/compounded semaglutide/tirzepatide, esp. novel forms (sublingual drops, nasal sprays).
- **Multi-state investigations** into DTC telehealth weight-loss platforms reported in **CA, TX, NY, MA** and others.
- **Corporate practice of medicine / telehealth structure:** several states bar non-clinician ownership of clinics and regulate telehealth advertising; "FDA-approved," "generic Ozempic," and equivalence claims are the recurring UDAP triggers. Every state's **UDAP statute** applies to supplement advertising generally.

## 6. PRACTICAL SYNTHESIS — CLAIM TRAFFIC LIGHTS + CONTENT RULES

### 6a. RUO peptide sellers
**Reality:** the market these clients want to serve (humans) is precisely the market that makes the product an **unapproved (often misbranded) new drug**. There is **no compliant way to market an RUO peptide *for human benefit*.** Content can only truthfully describe a laboratory reagent.

| 🟢 Allowed (genuine lab-reagent framing) | 🟡 Risky (likely intended-use evidence) | 🔴 Prohibited (creates unapproved-drug intent) |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical name, CAS #, purity/COA, molecular weight, storage/handling | Any before/after imagery; lifestyle/gym/wellness photos | "For weight loss / fat loss / appetite suppression / glucose control" |
| "For laboratory research use only; not for human consumption" (as the *only* framing) | FAQs that answer human-use questions | Dosing, titration, "cycle," or **reconstitution** instructions |
| Certificate of analysis, third-party testing of the *chemical* | Comparisons to approved drugs ("like Ozempic") | Injury/tendon/gut "repair," "healing," "recovery," anti-aging "reversal" |
| B2B/lab audience targeting | Blog "mechanism of action in humans" content | Customer **testimonials** about human results — including **liking/reposting** them |
| | Selling to consumer accounts | Bundling **bac water / syringes / injection kits** with the vial |
| | | Influencer content describing personal use |

### 6b. Supplement / GLP-1-adjacent wellness brands

| 🟢 Allowed (S/F, substantiated) | 🟡 Risky (borderline/implied disease or comparison) | 🔴 Prohibited (disease/drug claims) |
|---|---|---|
| "Supports healthy metabolism" | "Weight loss" (vs "weight management") | "Treats obesity," "clinically proven weight loss" |
| "Helps maintain a healthy weight as part of a balanced diet" | "Nature's Ozempic," "GLP-1 dupe" | "Same as / alternative to Ozempic/Wegovy/semaglutide" |
| "Supports normal appetite / satiety" | "Boosts your GLP-1" (Lemme risk if implying drug-like effect) | "Cures/treats diabetes," "lowers blood sugar" |
| "Promotes healthy skin / supports post-exercise recovery" | "Clinically studied ingredients" (establishment-claim risk unless finished product tested) | "Heals injury," "repairs joints/gut," "reverses aging" |
| NAD+/NMN "supports cellular energy" (S/F) | Weight-loss numbers ("lose 15 lbs") without RCT on the product | Any disease name as a target |

### 6c. Concrete content rules (apply to ALL channels — ads, organic, influencer, email, web)
1. **RUO peptides: no human-benefit content, period** — no depiction of consumption/injection, no dosing/reconstitution, no human-result testimonials, no lifestyle imagery, no "protocols," no bundling of injection supplies. The RUO disclaimer does not cure these.
2. **No equivalence/generic claims** for any compounded GLP-1 ("same active ingredient," "generic Ozempic," "same results").
3. **Substantiate before you publish** every objective claim with competent & reliable scientific evidence (ideally human RCTs on the finished product). Avoid **establishment/"clinically proven"** claims unless provable for the product as sold.
4. **DSHEA disclaimer** on all supplement S/F claims — a **floor, not a shield.** File the 30-day S/F notification.
5. **Influencer/endorsement disclosures must be "unavoidable"** and platform-native: spoken + on-screen in video, persistent in livestreams, superimposed in Stories; **#ad** up front, not buried. **Brand tags = endorsements.** The **brand/agency is liable** for the influencer's claims and disclosure failures.
6. **No fake or non-genuine reviews/testimonials, no review suppression, no bought engagement.** Insider reviews must disclose the connection. Before/after photos must be from **real customers who used the product.**
7. **No "FDA approved" claims** for compounded or RUO products; no implied completed clinical trials.
8. **A disclaimer never cures a non-compliant creative** — regulators read the total impression (imagery, product name, metatags, testimonials, likes), not just the fine print.
