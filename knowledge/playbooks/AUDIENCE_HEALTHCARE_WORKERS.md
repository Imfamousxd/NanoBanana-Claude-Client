## 2. The pain reality

- Registered nurses suffer **220.9 injuries and illnesses per 10,000 full-time workers versus 112.9
  across all private industry** — roughly double, and among the highest of any US profession.
- Shift work drives chronic sleep debt. **Black women report short sleep at 37% versus 27% of white
  women**, and the largest Black–white sleep gap sits inside professional occupations — a meaningful
  share of this segment carries compounded risk.

Injured, sleep-deprived, and clinically literate. No other segment has that combination, and the
third attribute is the commercial unlock.

## 3. The strategic advantage

**They read documents for a living.** A nurse can read a Certificate of Analysis without being
taught. This is the best audience in the catalogue for the "cast the verifier" strategy, because the
proof asset needs no translation.

The strongest hook in the entire knowledge base is built on it:

> **"If you can read an ABG, you can read a Certificate of Analysis."**

Three jobs in six words — it flatters real expertise, transfers a known skill onto an unfamiliar
document, and makes the brand's differentiator instantly legible.

**Identity line:** *"I take care of everyone else and I'm running on nothing."*

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| nights · rotating · off-going · charting · unit · floor | wellness journey · self-care Sunday |
| burnt out · running on fumes · circadian wreck | anxiety · insomnia · treat · therapy *(disease claims)* |
| lab sheet · assay · purity · lot · chain of custody | miracle · fix · cure |
| I'm not giving medical advice | as a nurse, I recommend *(license risk)* |

## 4. Content formats

NurseTok rewards in-group specificity, night-shift humour, and content that makes shift work feel
survivable — that last lane is the strategic home for the sleep and recovery SKUs.

1. **3 a.m. lull confessional.** Break room, whispered, 15s. Enormous send rate.
2. **"Things nobody prepped me for in nursing school."** Recurring series format.
3. **The ABG hook.** Reading a COA on camera, applying clinical literacy to a consumer purchase.
4. **Post-shift reality.** 8 a.m., blackout curtains, sunglasses, the drive home.
5. **Injury taxonomy by unit.** "ICU backs. ED shoulders. Med-surg feet." Pure in-group forwarding.
6. **Vendor-vetting for clinicians.** "You'd never hang a bag without checking the label. Same rule."

## 6. Visual and audio spec

- Break room, car in the parking garage, home at sunrise with blackout curtains. Scrubs are the
  costume and do enormous identity work — keep them in frame.
- Low light is correct and credible. Do not light it properly.

- Burn captions in — much of this is watched silently in a break room or a dark house.

## 7. Elevated compliance risk specific to this segment

1. **Licensure.** A licensed clinician endorsing a product carries a higher FTC substantiation bar,
   and their board can act on claims. Every clinician-creator asset gets legal review, and the
   phrase "as a nurse, I recommend" is banned outright.
2. **Sleep vocabulary.** *Insomnia, anxiety and depression are diagnosable conditions.* Any claim
   against them is a drug claim. DSIP content may only describe **sleep-wake cycle modulation
   research** — never relief, never treatment, never "knocks me out." Use pre-written caption
   blocks; no free-form copy on sleep SKUs.
