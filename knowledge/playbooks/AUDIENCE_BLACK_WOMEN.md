# Audience playbook — Black women, 35–55 (NuLumin)

**Priority Segment B.** Paired with `AUDIENCE_TRADES_MEN.md` (Segment A). Distribution physics,

## 1. Why this segment

- Non-Hispanic Black women have the **highest obesity prevalence of any US race/sex group — 56.9%**
  age-standardised. Black adults exceed 35% prevalence in **38 of 48** measured states and DC.
- Yet racial and ethnic minorities are **less likely to be prescribed GLP-1s even when insured**,
  **40% of GLP-1 prescriptions go unfilled**, and uninsured patients paid **$900+/month** in 2024.
- Black buying power reaches **$2.1 trillion** in 2026, and Black consumers **34% more likely** to
  pay more for a product that reflects their personal image.

**Highest need, worst access, real spending power.** That combination does not exist anywhere else
in the catalogue.

## 3. Four needs, not one — and the recommended entry is not the obvious one

This audience has four distinct high-prevalence needs with four different risk profiles. Treating
them as one "weight" segment is the biggest available mistake.

| Lane | The data | SKUs | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hair & scalp** | CCCA is the most common scarring hair loss in women of African descent, typically appearing in the 30s–40s. **40.9% self-report symptoms consistent with CCCA; only 8.8% have ever been diagnosed.** 29% report scalp sensitivity from chemical or tension styling. | GHK-Cu, Glow Blend | **Low** |
| **Perimenopause** | Menopause arrives **~2 years earlier** (SWAN median ~49). Perimenopause lasts **~10 years versus 6.5** for white women. Longer hot flashes, worse sleep disruption, higher depression/anxiety/stroke risk — **and less likely to receive hormone therapy.** | KissPeptin, Epithalon, DSIP | Low–Med |
| **Energy & sleep** | **37% report short sleep versus 27%** of white women; the largest Black–white sleep gap sits inside professional occupations. | NAD+, MOTS-C, DSIP | Low–Med |
| **Metabolic** | 56.9% obesity prevalence; the access gap above | GLP-1(S), GLP-3(R), AOD-9604 | **Highest** |

### 3.1 Lead with hair. It is the wedge.

Counterintuitive but the data is unambiguous:

- **Black women spend ~$1,500 a year on hair care — roughly six times more than other ethnic
  groups.** The Black haircare market is $8.85B in 2026, growing 6% CAGR.
- **A 40.9% symptom rate against an 8.8% diagnosis rate** is an enormous, un-named, unaddressed
  problem. Naming a real thing accurately is the single fastest way to earn trust with an audience
  that has been dismissed.
- GHK-Cu is a copper-binding tripeptide with an existing consumer vocabulary — "copper peptides"
  already circulate in beauty content, so there is no category education tax.
- **Zero enforcement heat** compared with GLP, and Glow Blend at $109.99 carries high AOV with
  approved studio and light-streak assets already shot.
- 54% of Black women now wear their natural hair, and the natural-hair creator ecosystem is mature,
  large, and used to detailed ingredient content.

**Sequence: hair → perimenopause and energy → metabolic.** The metabolic lane is fully specified in
§9; it is a wave-three move, not a launch move.

## 4. The trust barrier — the most important section in this document

- **55%** of Black Americans report negative experiences with doctors — having to speak up to get
  proper care, or having their pain dismissed.
- **51%** believe the US healthcare system was designed to hold Black people back.
- But **67.4% say they trust their own primary care provider.** The mistrust is *institutional, not
  interpersonal*.
- Documented driver of mistrust: **dismissiveness.** Documented drivers of trust: **mutual respect,
  open communication, and feeling like "more than just a number."**

**The strategic consequence.** This audience's barrier is not skepticism about peptides. It is
skepticism about being sold to and being dismissed. NuLumin's proof stack — batch-specific COA,
HPLC and mass spec, lot traceability — is literally *"don't take my word for it, here is the
paper."* That maps precisely onto the documented driver of trust.

> **Never "trust me." Always "don't trust me — here's the document."**

That posture is simultaneously the compliance-safe position and the culturally correct one. It is
the whole reason this segment is winnable.

## 5. Cultural authenticity — what the data actually requires

- **67%** of Black consumers pay more attention to advertising that reflects their culture, versus
  46% of the general population.
- **71%** say they feel misrepresented in media; **61%** specifically in advertising.
- **85%** favour brands that feel culturally authentic; **42%** will switch for better representation.
- Younger Black consumers spot performative allyship instantly, having watched brands fumble it for
  a decade.

**Operating rules that follow:**

1. **Cast Black creators. Black hands in every product shot.** Not one token frame in a rotation.
2. **Year-round or not at all.** A February-only activation reads as exactly what it is.
3. **The creator carries the cultural voice. The brand does not code-switch.** NuLumin's register
   stays clinical-modern — that is the locked identity and it is also what signals respect. A
   research brand suddenly speaking AAVE in its own captions is the textbook fumble. Let the
   creator sound like herself; let the brand sound like a lab.
4. **No inspiration-porn framing.** She is not "overcoming." She is buying a product and checking
   the paperwork.

## 6. The stigma trap — the finding that shapes the creative

A 2026 study found women who lost weight using GLP-1 medications were **judged more harshly than
women who lost weight through diet and exercise**, driven by the belief that medication-assisted
loss is a "shortcut." Accusation content — *"did she take something?"* — has become pervasive across
social platforms and is escalating in venom. (Notably, measured stigma was *higher* when the woman
was portrayed as white than Black, but the accusation dynamic reaches everyone.)

**Consequence: transformation content is a liability here on cultural grounds and compliance grounds
simultaneously.** Your rule against bodies, scales and before/afters is not a creative handicap in
this segment — it is the correct cultural strategy. Content that never shows a body cannot trigger
the accusation cycle.

This is a rare alignment between what the law requires and what the audience actually wants.
Exploit it deliberately and say so in briefs.

## 8. Content formats and hook bank

### Formats

1. **The receipt.** Numbers on screen: $900/month uninsured versus catalogue price, with the
   documentation difference explained. Cost is a legal conversation; outcomes are not.
2. **The COA read-along.** Pull the certificate, match the lot number, read it aloud. Doubles as SEO.
3. **"They didn't tell us."** Health-information-gap content. Entirely factual, deeply resonant,
   zero product claims. Highest forward rate of any format in this segment.
4. **Naming the thing.** CCCA and perimenopause explainers — giving a real name to an experience
   she has had for years without a word for it.
5. **Kitchen-table long caption.** Facebook-native, 200–400 words, conversational. Cheap and it
   converts in the 45+ half.
6. **Vendor literacy.** "Before you give anybody your card number, ask for these four things."

### Hook bank — all claim-free, pre-cleared against §7 of the mechanics doc

**Hair**
- "Forty percent of us have symptoms of a specific kind of hair loss. Under nine percent have ever been told what it's called."
- "We spend six times more on our hair than anybody else and get the least information about it."
- "Nobody said the word CCCA to me until I was forty-one."
- "Copper peptides. What they actually are, and what the research is looking at."
- "Your edges aren't a personal failing. There's a name for this."

**Perimenopause and sleep**
- "We hit this two years earlier and it lasts ten years instead of six and a half. Nobody mentions that part."
- "My doctor said I was too young. The research says I'm right on time."
- "Thirty-seven percent of us are running on short sleep. Twenty-seven percent of white women. That gap isn't an accident."

**Access and cost**
- "Nine hundred dollars a month uninsured. Let me show you the actual paperwork on the other option."
- "Forty percent of these prescriptions never get filled. Here's why."

**Verification**
- "Don't trust me. Trust the lot number. Let me show you how to check it."
- "Every time somebody sells us something, ask for the paper. Here's what the paper should say."
- "If they can't show you a certificate with a matching lot number, close the tab."

## 9. Visual direction — and an honest asset gap

- Warm, clean, real. **Not** the raw film-grit register used for Segment A — this audience's
  aesthetic reference sits closer to beauty and clinical-modern.
- Still no HDR-AI look. Still no polished commercial gloss.
- **Black hands in every product shot. Non-negotiable.**
- Real hair textures — natural, protective styles, locs, relaxed, wigs. All of it, not one look.
- Settings: bathroom mirror, kitchen counter, car before work, salon chair, porch at night.
- **Never body-focused framing.** Face, hands, hair, product, document. Per §6, this is a hard rule.

**Existing assets that work:** approved Glow/Klow studio and light-streak heroes
(`NuLumin Send/Glow & Klow/`), `NuLumin Generated/White BG/` packshots.

**The gap, stated plainly:** the existing 72-shot themed lifestyle library was generated against a
brief that skewed young and did not specify this audience. **It will not serve Segment B.** This
segment requires a dedicated shoot — roughly 20–24 frames covering hair/scalp, bathroom-mirror
routine, kitchen table, and salon, with Black women 35–55 across a range of hair textures and body
types, shot above the shoulders or hands-and-product only. Budget for it; do not try to retrofit
the existing set.

## 12. The metabolic lane — hard rules if and when you run it

FDA warning letters specifically named unapproved GLP-1 receptor agonists introduced with evidence
of human-use intent despite RUO labelling. TikTok prohibits "GLP" in product branding or title and
bans weight-management claims. Meta will not run it.

- Never name or compare to Ozempic, Wegovy, Zepbound or Mounjaro
- Never show a body, a scale, a tape measure, a clothing-size comparison, or a before/after
- Never use *weight loss, fat loss, appetite, cravings, slimming, transformation*
- Never list a metabolic SKU on TikTok Shop
- Use the compliant names **GLP-1(S), GLP-2(T), GLP-3(R)**
- Every metabolic asset gets legal review, including organic gifting posts
- The only permitted frame is **access, price, supply chain and documentation** — never outcome

## 13. Two lines that are specific to this segment

**Never use race as a claim mechanism.** "Formulated for Black women's metabolism" is both
scientifically unsupported and a product claim. The disparity data is *why you are talking to this
audience* — it is never *what the product fixes*. Keep those two ideas surgically separate in every
brief.

**Never imply the product closes a health gap.** Naming a documented disparity is factual and
appropriate. Positioning a research compound as its remedy is a disease claim wearing a social-
justice costume, and it is the fastest way to lose both the FDA and the audience.
